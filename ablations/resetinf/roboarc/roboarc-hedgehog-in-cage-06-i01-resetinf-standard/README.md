## Task
Standard-mode twin of `roboarc-hedgehog-in-cage-06-i01-privileged` (same frozen instance): the agent gets only the `eai-standard/2.4` client
(docs/protocol/standard_mode.md); the simulator runs in the `sim` sidecar (`environment/docker-compose.yaml`, GPU on the
sidecar), which owns the scene and records the episode as a (T, 8) trajectory; its collect hook
(`environment/sim/finalize.sh`) writes the live episode's verdict, then renders the video in one video-only
replay of the recorded rows (no grading replay). The verifier runs in a separate sandbox (`tests/Dockerfile`) over the sidecar's artifacts. Compare against
the privileged task to measure the cost of losing resets, privileged state and the tooling's primitives.

## Difficulty
Same scene, instance and success rule as `roboarc-hedgehog-in-cage-06-i01-privileged` (see its Difficulty). In standard mode the agent loses resets, privileged state and planning primitives: it sees camera images and proprioception only, acts through the client, and gets one episode, so it has to estimate the scene from images and recover from its own mistakes without starting over.

## Reference solution
`solution/solve.sh` streams the recorded rows of the standard-mode oracle episode (`solution/oracle_qpos.npy`, one `joint_position` row per 25 ms control step, `hold`; the same rows as the privileged reference) through the client (`solution/oracle_replay.py`). That episode scored 1 (live and fresh replay) on the release image efe:roboarc-0.1.0 (2026-10-14; simulator source 37aa7ac0, tooling d8692e15).

## Verification
See `tests/grade.py`.

`nop` scored 0 on this base in both modes (the release image efe:roboarc-0.1.0; harbor run -a nop with rendering off; 2026-10-14).

## Details

### Goal
Separate and place the whole star, including every spike inside the green dashed goal region. Keep all cage components on the puzzle/system side. A thin rod with a box handle lies on the table; you may use it as a tool. It is a task piece: if it falls off the table (more than 100 mm below the tabletop), the episode ends in failure.

### Success requirements
- Put every part of the whole star, including every spike inside the fixed goal volume, leaving at least 1 mm inside its horizontal border. The centre alone is insufficient; no tip, spike, ring segment or cord segment may remain outside.
- Keep the target above the tabletop (contact tolerance 1 mm below the surface) and within the goal volume's height. It need not touch the table. The goal volume extends 237.284 mm above the tabletop.
- Keep all cage components, and every other remaining puzzle component, entirely on the system side of the fixed separator. Moving the whole puzzle into the goal is not completion.
- Keep each target rigid part's linear speed at most 1 mm/s and angular speed at most 0.02 rad/s. Holding the target with the gripper is allowed; release is not required.
- Keep all final conditions satisfied together for at least 2 s of simulation time. If any final condition stops holding, the confirmation interval starts again. A brief qualifying pose is not enough; let the simulation advance through the full interval.

### Task failure conditions
- A task piece falling more than 100 mm below the tabletop ends the episode in failure. Finger contact with a held movable piece may penetrate at most 2 mm; holding requires both fingers of the same hand to contact the same piece, or an active assisted grasp by that hand. All other task contacts, including piece-piece, table, arm and gripper-housing contacts, may penetrate at most 1 mm. Exceeding either limit ends the episode in failure.

### Grader details (in addition to the requirements above)
- Goal square in this instance: the inner edge of the 2 mm wide dashed green line is the containment limit (the region's border runs along the middle of the line); it encloses a 245.3 mm x 245.3 mm square. Every point counts: for a rigid part every vertex of its visual and collision meshes.
- The separator is an invisible, fixed vertical plane parallel to the goal square's near edge, 5 mm beyond the point of all remaining components that was closest to the goal square at the start; the square's near edge is at least 30 mm beyond it. The remaining components here are fixed.
- Contact penetration as this benchmark grades it (it replaces the "at most 1 mm / 2 mm" wording above): penetration is recorded, it never ends the episode by itself. A success with deep interpenetration (a task contact deeper than 3 mm, or deeper than 2 mm for at least 0.25 s: 100 consecutive physics ticks; for a finger contact with a held piece 4 mm, or 2 mm for 0.25 s) is not credited automatically but reviewed, with the recorded evidence and frames; a clean success is credited automatically. A task contact deeper than 10 mm is a simulator breakdown (below).
- Contact penetration is read from the contacts at the end of every physics tick (2.5 ms; 10 ticks per control step). "Held" is decided anew at every tick: a zero-force touch counts, and if one finger loses contact (with no active assisted grasp), the other finger is back at the non-held limits in the same tick. The cage is fixed, so finger contact with it is never held (always the non-held limits).
- Robot safety (this benchmark's tooling): no gripper target goes lower than 15 mm below the tabletop; a commanded target that would bring any part of a gripper lower is stopped there, on the commanded path. The fingertips reach about 6 mm beyond the tool frame (TCP) along its approach axis when open and about 19 mm when closed (the Robotiq fingertips move forward as they close). Arm drive torques are capped (the effort limits in robot_spec), so an arm pushing against an obstacle stalls at that torque. An arm with any part more than 5 mm deep in a fixed object (a fixture, the table) is stopped where it is: its joint targets become its measured joints for that control step, so it stops pushing (the response does not report these stops).
- Forcing is recorded, not judged: an arm drive (not a gripper drive) at 95% of its torque cap or more while that arm's gripper, or a part it holds, presses on anything else with 20 N or more in total is forcing; 0.5 s of it on every physics tick is a forcing event in the evidence (`meta["safety"]`), shown to the reviewer when a success is reviewed. Forcing does not end the episode and does not change the grade by itself.
- Simulator breakdown (a non-finite state or a task contact deeper than 10 mm): the episode ends as an infrastructure error, not your failure, unless an arm was forcing (even for one tick) within the 0.25 s before it: then it is your failure (forced_breakdown: what broke and what forced it).
- Grasp regions (assisted grasping, see Grippers above; one sphere per graspable part): hedgehog_ball: radius 90.1 mm, enclosing the whole part; push_rod: radius 37.1 mm, around the rod's box handle. Both finger contacts must lie inside the part's sphere; a part touched elsewhere is grasped physically.
