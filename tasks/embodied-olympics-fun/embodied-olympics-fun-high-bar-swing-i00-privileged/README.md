## Task
Standing on the block behind the high bar, jump up and catch the bar with both hands, then do one giant swing: take your extended body in a full circle around the bar, without letting go. (Embodied Olympics `high-bar-swing`: one Unitree G1 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
A real jump: the legs must lift the whole robot about 0.15 m and forwards so that the bar enters the open hands, and the fingers must close round it in flight. Then nothing pushes you: the swing has to be pumped up from a hang by the legs, the waist and the shoulders, against motors of 88 N m at the hips and 25 N m in the arms, until the body goes over the top of the bar extended; the bottom of a giant swing pulls about four times your weight through the fingers and the arms, and the hands slide round the fixed bar against their own friction.

## Reference solution
None. No reference controller exists for this family yet: positive by a declared magic action (the G1 on a 1.1 m block behind the bar; its own Dex3-1 hands, drives 5 times the device's, declared: the one exception to the benchmark's 2x cap; no weld): the jump is one injected velocity (a 0.18 m rise), the hands close 0.12 s before the top and catch the bar, then an injected spin as from 60 deg and a gravity-like drive of at most 400 N m about the bar (no joint loaded by it): a full extended circle in 4.95 s, bottom load up to 8.6 body weights in 10 ms samples, all held by the hands' own drives; the same state hash twice. The grip-force need, the same magic action: 4 and 4.5 times let go, 5 and 6 times hold, forwards and backwards; capacity of the two hands on the bar: about 1 body weight at the device's torques (the catch tears them off), 1.55 at 1.5 times, 2.05 at 2 times. Negatives: nop (time), no catch (hands open: the mats), holding the bar without a jump, the hands opened (let go), a fall from the block (the mats), a tucked circle and a circle that turns back (referee-only rotations: no count). Stress: random joint targets on the bar then the hands opened: let go; the finger links press at most 3.3 mm into the bar..

## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in one fresh process (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success. The rules: The bar is a competition horizontal bar: 0.028 m thick, 2.8 m above the floor, 2.4 m long between its two uprights, along the y axis; it is fixed and does not turn. Your hands are three-finger hands (a thumb, an index and a middle finger; each hand's drives 5.0 times the stock hand's torques); they hold the bar by their fingers' contact and friction only, so the bar can be torn out of a hand. A hand grasps the bar while the bar touches its index or middle finger and its thumb or palm; a hand is on the bar while the bar touches any part of it (fingers, thumb or palm: a hook grip counts). You start standing on the block and must jump to the bar: the catch is the moment both hands grasp the bar after you have left the block (grasping it before leaving the block fails). Before the catch you fail if you touch the mats or the floor; you may land back on the block and jump again. After the catch, when for more than 0.05 s a hand has not been on the bar you have let go (whether you opened the hand or the bar was torn out), and there is no regrip; only the hands may touch the bar; touching the block, the uprights, the mats or the floor fails. Your swing angle is the angle between straight down and the line from the bar to your centre of mass, seen from the side. The giant swing is a full circle of that line after the catch: the angle must travel 360.0 degrees in one direction (forwards or backwards) without turning back; a turn back of more than 5.0 degrees starts the count again from where you turned. The body must be extended over the top: whenever your centre of mass is higher than the bar it must be at least 0.59 m from the midpoint of your two wrists (nine tenths of the dead hang's: a slight pike or arch passes, a tuck or bent arms do not); if it comes closer there, the count starts again from that point. You succeed the moment the count reaches 360.0 degrees. Below the bar you may bend as you like to build up the swing. You fail after 60 s. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §4).
