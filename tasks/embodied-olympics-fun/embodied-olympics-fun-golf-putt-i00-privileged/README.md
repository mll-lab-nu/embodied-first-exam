## Task
Hole the 3.0 m putt: with one stroke of the putter held in your right hand, roll the golf ball that lies on the green into the hole, without letting go of the putter. (Embodied Olympics `golf-putt`: one Unitree G1 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
The hole is two and a half ball widths across and three metres away: the ball must start within about one degree of the line and at a speed between the one that just reaches the hole and the one that jumps it, and there is no second stroke. The club is an adult's, long and heavy for this robot's wrist, and it has to be brought from where it starts to an address beside the ball first. It is held across the palm of a three-finger hand by friction: levered it turns in the fingers, so it has to be carried with the shaft near the vertical, and the swing rocks it in the hand, so the face at impact is not quite where the arm put it.

## Reference solution
`solution/oracle.py` drives the robot through the task's own tooling, closed loop on the privileged state, and writes the trajectory (`solution/solve.sh`). Reference for the G1's own Dex3-1 right hand at the device's torques (the putter's grip across the palm, held by the closed fingers and thumb; no weld): a one-armed pendulum putt by the wrist's roll joint with the wrist's yaw held at zero, the arm's kinematics reading where the club is in the hand; holed at 18.0 s of the 30 s limit (one hand: with the club soled behind the ball, inverse kinematics from 40 starts finds no collision-free posture with both hands on the grip, the misses 5 cm and more or the arms through the trunk), one 12 ms contact of the head; the same state hash twice; holed again with the swing 2 % faster and with the ball 2 mm towards the heel or the toe (3 of 5 perturbations; a face closed 0.5 degree more or a take-back 0.02 rad longer stops 6-10 cm short). At the start the hanging club turns 0.7 degree in the hand in 3 s. Negatives: nop (time), the hand opened (the putter left the hand), injected rolls too soft, too hard, over the edge, past the hole, at rest on the lip, off the green, a push, a double hit then a second stroke, a ball thrown at the shaft, a kick, astride the line, a fall; stimp 10 measured. Stress: random arm targets with the club in the hand end on the anchoring rule at 0.34 s, the club at most 7.4 mm into anything. Measured: success after 17.99 s of the 30 s limit, live and in a fresh replay, ending in the same state.

## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in one fresh process (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success. The rules: The ball (42.67 mm, 45.9 g) lies at rest on a flat green of stimp 10 (a ball that starts rolling at 1.83 m/s stops after 3.05 m). The hole is cut into the green 3.0 m from the ball: 0.108 m across and 0.1016 m deep, with no flagstick in it. A putter (0.86 m long, 0.5 kg, 3.0 degrees of loft, a round grip 0.024 m thick and 0.26 m long) is a separate body; at the start its grip lies across the palm of your right hand (a three-finger hand: a thumb, an index and a middle finger), the fingers closed round it, which holds it by contact and friction only: it can slip, turn in the hand and fall. The putter is in your hand while its grip touches the hand's index or middle finger and its thumb or palm; it has left your hand when for more than 0.05 s it has not. Your left hand may hold the grip too; the putter may touch nothing of you but your two hands. You succeed when the ball is holed with one stroke: after the stroke it comes to rest (its centre moves less than 0.001 m in 1.0 s) inside the hole with the whole ball below the surface of the green. A stroke is a fair strike of the ball with the putter's head (any side of the head): the first contact of the head with the ball starts it, every further contact must come within 0.2 s of the first (an accidental double hit is still one stroke), and all the contacts together may last at most 0.03 s. You fail if the putter leaves your right hand at any time (dropped, thrown or slipped out of the hand); if the putter touches any part of you other than your hands; if the contacts last longer (the ball was pushed, scraped or scooped), if the head touches the ball again later (a second stroke), if anything else of you ever touches the ball (the putter's shaft or grip, a foot, a leg, a hand), if at the moment of the stroke your feet touch the green on both sides of the line of play (the straight line through the ball's spot and the hole's centre, extended behind the ball) or on that line, if the ball comes to rest anywhere but in the hole (on the lip and on the green beyond the hole included), if the ball leaves the green, if you fall (your pelvis lower than 0.35 m above the green, or any part of you other than your feet touching the ground; the putter may touch the green as long as it stays in your hand), or after 30 s.
