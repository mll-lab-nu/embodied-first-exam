## Task
Serve: from behind your baseline, release the tennis ball in your left hand, strike it with the racket on your right hand before it reaches the ground, and send it over the net into the service box diagonally opposite. (Embodied Olympics `tennis-serve`; the robot as a service: its head cameras, joint sensors and IMUs, and the venue's fixed camera only.)

## Difficulty
The ball must be struck in the air with the strings of a racket that reaches up to 2.7 m, leave at well over 10 m/s in a narrow window of directions to clear a net 11.9 m away and still come down within 6.4 m beyond it, and the swing that gives it that speed must not knock over or move the feet of a body that stands without a balance controller. Both arms have to work in time: the released ball is in the racket's reach for a few tenths of a second. In standard mode the agent has no simulator and no state: it sees through the robot's cameras, acts through the client, and has 50 resets (only the last attempt is graded).

## Reference solution
None (as `embodied-olympics-fun-tennis-serve-i00-privileged`).

## Verification
The sim sidecar's referee judges the live attempt, and that is the verdict (`final.json`: `graded_by` live, `replays` 0;
no grading replay, owner 2026-10-17, board 0151); the frozen start is checked on the live simulator (`instance_mismatch`:
infrastructure), and the video is rendered from the recorded attempt afterwards. `/tests/grade.py` reads
`/data/final.json`: pass iff the live attempt succeeded. The
rules: The court is the ITF singles court: 23.77 m long and 8.23 m wide, measured to the outside of the lines, with a service line 6.4 m from the net on each side and a centre service line that divides the space between the net and each service line into two service boxes. The net stands across the middle, 0.914 m high at its centre and rising in a straight line to 1.07 m at the posts, which stand 0.914 m outside the sidelines. The ball is the ITF ball (radius 0.0335 m, 0.0577 kg, with air drag and spin lift) and starts gripped in your left hand; the racket (0.685 m long, 0.30 kg, strung surface 0.33 m by 0.25 m) is fixed to your right hand. You serve from the right of the centre mark into the far service box on the left of the centre line. In order: (1) the toss: you let go of the ball (the grip takes a ball only once) while you stand at rest, both feet on the ground behind the baseline, between the extension of the centre mark and the extension of the right sideline, neither foot having left the ground or moved more than 0.02 m in the last 0.5 s (so not before 0.5 s after the start); (2) the strike: your racket hits the ball before it reaches the ground (any part of the racket counts), the ball having touched nothing of you for the last 0.1 s and moving at most 5.0 m/s when it reaches the racket (a toss, not a throw), and the hit speeds the ball up (0.05 s after its last contact with the racket it is faster than it was when it reached the racket); (3) the ball's centre passes the net's plane between the posts above the net's top edge; (4) its first contact with the ground is inside that service box, the lines included (the point under the ball's centre decides; ground contacts less than 0.02 s apart are one): that is the success. From the release to the strike your feet may not touch the baseline or the court, the ground beyond the sideline's extension or the centre mark's extension, nor the ground more than 0.3 m from where each stood at the release (a foot fault); after the strike you may step anywhere on your side. A serve that touches the net and still lands in the box is a let: it does not count, and you serve again, under the same rules, with the spare ball (your left hand's grip can take it from then on); this happens at most 1 time(s), a further let fails. You fail on any fault: releasing the ball when not at rest in the server's position, a foot fault, the tossed ball reaching the ground or passing the net unstruck, the racket striking the ball before it is clear of you, a ball that reaches the racket faster than 5.0 m/s, a hit that does not speed it up, the racket touching the struck ball again (contacts less than 0.05 s apart are one hit) or staying on it longer than 0.05 s, the served ball touching you, a net post, the ground on your side or the ground outside the service box, or passing the net's plane anywhere but over the net. You also fail if any part of you or the racket touches the net or a post, if you fall, or after 20 s. A fall is: your pelvis lower than 0.35 m above the ground, or any part of you other than your feet touching the ground; the racket may touch the ground. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §2.7).
