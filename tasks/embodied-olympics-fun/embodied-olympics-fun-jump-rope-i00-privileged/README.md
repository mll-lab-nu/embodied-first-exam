## Task
Skip rope: turn the rope forwards with both hands, over your head and under your feet, and jump it with both feet together, 17 times within one minute. (Embodied Olympics `jump-rope`: one Unitree G1 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
Every jump needs two skills at once. The arms must keep a light, floppy rope turning at a steady speed through hands that hold it only by friction. The legs must make a two-footed hop, about 0.1 m high and timed to within a tenth of a second, to the rope coming under the feet. Then the robot must land and do it again 17 times without falling. Neither built-in gait can hop, and a rope that stops on a shin or a toe has to be started again from behind.

## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in one fresh process (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success. The rules: You hold a skipping rope by its two handles, one in each of your three-finger hands. Turn it forwards: over your head from back to front, then down in front of you and under your feet from front to back. A jump counts when the rope, after passing over your head, passes under both feet while nothing of you touches the floor, and your take-off was two-footed: both feet left the floor within 0.1 s of each other. The rope passes over your head when its point straight above your head crosses the upright plane through your head, from back to front, higher than the top of your head. It passes under a foot when its point at that foot's sideways position crosses the upright plane through that foot, across your heading, from front to back, lower than the bottom of the foot plus the rope's thickness, while it moves backwards over the floor at 1.0 m/s or more (the rope turning, not you hopping forwards over a still rope). It must pass under the second foot within 0.15 s of the first. Each turn of the rope counts at most once. Extra hops between turns are allowed but do not count. A miss does not end the attempt: if the rope stops on your body, passes under the feet while a foot or anything else of you touches the floor, passes under only one foot, turns backwards, or comes under your feet without first going over your head, that turn does not count, and the rope must go over your head again before the next jump. After your 17th counted jump you must land on your feet (the first thing of you to touch the floor) and then stand for 1.0 s without a break: only your feet on the floor, your torso within about 20 degrees of vertical, your pelvis slower than 0.35 m/s; then you succeed. You fail if you let go of a handle (a handle out of its hand for more than 0.05 s: not touching that hand's fingers and its thumb or palm; or a handle touching the floor), if the last landing is not on your feet, if you fall (your pelvis lower than 0.35 m, or any part of you other than your feet on the floor), or after 60 s. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §4).

The family's success line is the standard's pass mark (60 points) for first-grade boys: 17 jumps in one minute; deviations: Equipment: the standard names no rope. The rope here is a 7 ft (2.13 m between the swivels) beaded school rope, the length that skipping-rope sizing charts give a skipper shorter than 1.45 m (4 ft 9 in): 28 rigid segments of two 38 mm low-density polyethylene beads each (9.5 mm across, Kanga's deluxe beaded rope), 0.1 kg in all (a 7 ft segmented rope weighs 0.2 lb). Neighbouring segments are tied by stiff pivots that stretch by a few millimetres under load (the cord); the rope feels the air's drag and collides with itself, the floor and the robot. Its two plastic handles are 0.15 m long and 0.04 kg each, with the rope on a swivel at their far ends. · Hands: the robot's hands are its own Unitree Dex3-1 three-finger hands (1.37 N m back-driven per finger joint, at the device's torques). Each handle lies across the palm, the fingers and the thumb closed round it, held only by their contact and friction, so it can slip out or be dropped. · Judging: a judge's eye is replaced by geometry, checked at every 1 ms physics step. The rope passes under a foot when its point at that foot's sideways position crosses, from front to back, the upright plane across the robot's heading through the foot, lower than the foot's lowest point (plus the rope's thickness). It passes over the head when its point at the head's sideways position crosses the upright plane through the head from back to front, higher than the top of the head. Three numbers the standard leaves to the judge's eye: the rope must move backwards at 1.0 m/s or more as it passes (a turning rope, not a hop over a still one), under the second foot within 0.15 s of the first, and both feet must leave the floor within 0.1 s of each other (a two-foot jump). · Timing: the minute runs from t = 0, with the robot standing, a handle in each hand and the rope on the floor behind its heels. The attempt ends with success once the robot has landed on its feet after the 17th jump and stood still for 1.0 s (the families' land-and-stand rule), without waiting for the end of the minute. |
