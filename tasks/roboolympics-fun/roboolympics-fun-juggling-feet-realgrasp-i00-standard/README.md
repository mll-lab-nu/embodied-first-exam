## Task
Juggle the football with your feet: drop it from between your palms and keep it in the air for ten foot touches in a row, each one sending the ball up above your knees, without it touching the ground or any other part of you. (RoboOlympics `juggling-feet-realgrasp`; the robot as a service: its head cameras, joint sensors and IMUs, and the venue's fixed camera only.)

## Difficulty
Each touch is an upward strike of a foot, timed to a falling 0.43 kg ball and aimed so that the ball comes down on a foot again, ten times in a row, and the robot's thick lower leg leaves only the front few centimetres of the foot where a falling ball meets the foot and not the shin, a spot where a few millimetres decide whether the ball goes up, forward or back onto the leg. To bring a foot under the ball the robot has to stand on the other leg, which neither built-in gait does, and a ball that merely bounces off a still foot loses most of its height and wanders off. In standard mode the agent has no simulator and no state: it sees through the robot's cameras, acts through the client, and has 50 resets (only the last attempt is graded).

## Reference solution
None (as `roboolympics-fun-juggling-feet-realgrasp-i00-privileged`).

## Verification
The sim sidecar's referee judges the live attempt, and that is the verdict (`final.json`: `graded_by` live, `replays` 0;
no grading replay, owner 2026-10-17, board 0151); the frozen start is checked on the live simulator (`instance_mismatch`:
infrastructure), and the video is rendered from the recorded attempt afterwards. `/tests/grade.py` reads
`/data/final.json`: pass iff the live attempt succeeded. The
rules: A size-5 football (0.22 m across, 0.43 kg, in air) starts pressed between your two open palms; you stand in the middle of a square grid 5.0 m on each side, marked on the grass. Your hands have no fingers and no grip (the grip columns of the action rows do nothing in this task): only the squeeze of your arms and friction hold the ball, and it slips out when they press too little. Until the drop only your hands (the palm pads and the wrists) may touch the ball. The drop: the ball has touched no part of you for 0.03 s; it is the end of the ball's last contact with a hand. A hand or an arm may still be in contact with the ball for 0.15 s after the drop (the ball leaving the hand); after that a hand or an arm on the ball is a handball. From the drop on, only your feet may touch the ball: a foot is the part below the ankle joint (sole, instep, sides, toe and heel); a contact with your lower leg also counts as the foot where it lies over the foot (from 0.08 m behind to 0.15 m ahead of the ankle joint along the foot) and no more than 0.12 m above the ankle joint (along the foot's up direction), because your leg's collision shape covers the instep; the leg above that (shin, knee, thigh), the trunk, the head and the arms are other surfaces. A touch is a contact of the ball with a foot (contacts less than 0.05 s apart are one touch). It counts when, before anything touches the ball again, the ball's centre has risen at least 0.22 m above the height it had when the touch began and the whole ball (its lowest point) has risen above 0.36 m, your knee height; and when the ball left the touch with at least 0.95 of the speed it had when the touch began (both taken at the height where the touch began): a ball that only bounces off a still foot leaves slower, and such a touch is not counted, and is not a fault. You succeed at the moment the tenth touch counts. You fail if the ball touches the ground (before the drop too), if a part of you other than the hands touches it before the drop, if a hand or an arm touches it later than 0.15 s after the drop, or at all while a foot touches it or before a foot's touch has counted or been judged a bounce, if any part of you other than a foot touches it, if both feet touch it at once (the ball trapped), if one touch lasts longer than 0.05 s (the ball resting, rolling or balanced on a foot), if the ball is touched again before a touch has counted or been judged a bounce, if a foot (its ankle) leaves the grid, if you fall (your pelvis lower than 0.35 m above the ground, or any part of you other than the feet on the ground; standing on one foot is not a fall), or after 60 s. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §2.7).

The G1's collision hull of the lower leg is wider and deeper than its shin and covers the instep: a ball lying on the top of the foot touches it. A contact with the lower leg's hull therefore counts as the foot where its point lies over the foot (between 0.08 m behind and 0.15 m ahead of the ankle joint along the foot) and at most 0.12 m above the ankle joint (measured along the foot's up direction: where the ball's equator is when it lies on the instep, plus 1 cm); a contact higher up the leg is the shin, the knee or the thigh: another surface, which the test's count does not allow. · A counter's eye is replaced by numbers: contacts less than 0.05 s apart are one touch, a touch may last at most 0.05 s (a juggle is a strike, the ball may not rest, be cushioned or be balanced on the foot; a real instep kick's contact lasts about 0.01 s, Nunome et al. 2006, J. Sports Sci. 24: 11-22, and the robot's strikes last 0.011-0.013 s), and a touch counts when the ball's centre has then risen at least 0.22 m (one ball diameter) above where it was when the touch began and the whole ball has risen above the robot's knee height (0.36 m above the ground standing), before anything touches it again. · A juggle is a strike: so that a ball that merely bounces off a still foot is not juggling, a touch must send the ball off with at least 0.95 of the speed it arrived with (a still foot returns at most the ball's own restitution, 0.85, and a raised leg held by its motors about 0.55); a touch that does not is not counted, and is not a fault. · The ball's contact with the foot keeps the ball's own restitution (0.85, from the rule book's drop test): the robot's foot is rigid, like the metal pendulum with which Andersen, Kristensen and Sorensen (Football Science 5: 45-50, 2008) measured 0.82, and the compliance of a human foot and ankle (whose kicks they and earlier studies measured at 0.45-0.65) is here the compliance of the robot's own joints: a raised leg held by its motors returns about 0.55 of the ball's speed. · The ball starts pressed between the robot's two open palms in front of its hips (the robot has flat palm pads, no fingers and no grip): only the arms' squeeze and friction hold it, and it slips out when they press too little. The drop is the hands' own movement (the palms parting); a referee's eye for 'the ball has left the hand' is replaced by a number: the ball touches no part of the robot for 0.03 s, and the drop is the end of its last contact. Before the drop only the hands may touch the ball (a ball held against the body, an arm or a leg is not in the hand). The palm absorbs the ball as a hand does (the palm-ball contact of dunk-realgrasp: a ball thrown at a still palm keeps 0.12 of its speed); every other contact keeps the ball's rule-book rebound.
