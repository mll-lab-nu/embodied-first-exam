## Task
Standard-mode twin of `roboarc-fiber-bundling-07-i01-privileged` (same frozen instance): the agent gets only the `eai-standard/2.4` client
(docs/protocol/standard_mode.md); the simulator runs in the `sim` sidecar (`environment/docker-compose.yaml`, GPU on the
sidecar), which owns the scene and records the episode as a (T, 8) trajectory; its collect hook
(`environment/sim/finalize.sh`) writes the live episode's verdict, then renders the video in one video-only
replay of the recorded rows (no grading replay). The verifier runs in a separate sandbox (`tests/Dockerfile`) over the sidecar's artifacts. Compare against
the privileged task to measure the cost of losing resets, privileged state and the tooling's primitives.

## Difficulty
Same scene, instance and success rule as `roboarc-fiber-bundling-07-i01-privileged` (see its Difficulty). In standard mode the agent loses resets, privileged state and planning primitives: it sees camera images and proprioception only, acts through the client, and gets one episode, so it has to estimate the scene from images and recover from its own mistakes without starting over.

## Reference solution
`solution/solve.sh` streams the rows of the standard reference (`solution/oracle_qpos.npy`, one `joint_position` row per control step, `hold`) through the client (`solution/oracle_replay.py`): the released reference moved an arm joint target more than the standard service's joint-speed rule (0.04 rad per control step) in some rows; these rows are the control steps the service executed for it (the live journal of a Harbor standard oracle trial), so the service runs every row as it is (scripts/roboarc/oracles/fiber-bundling-07-i01/standard.npz, meta speed_rule; the privileged reference is unchanged). That episode scored 1 (live and fresh replay, end state `68db8dfdc178b49c`) on the release image efe:roboarc-0.1.0, which holds the same frozen instance and reference rows (efe:roboarc-0.2.0 adds speed cuts in the simulator's host code only; its physics is bit-identical) (2026-10-14; simulator source 37aa7ac0, tooling d8692e15).

## Details

### Goal
Pass every fine fiber and the front sheath through the fixed copper ring; keep the rear terminal anchored.

### Success requirements
- Pass every fiber tip first, then successive material points through to its root. The actual crossing history must be observed; placing fibers on the far side or passing only the tips is not completion.
- All fiber points must finish beyond the exit side of the ring tube, allowing for fiber thickness. The branching face of the front sheath must also pass through and clear the tube with its full thickness. The fixed rear terminal must remain anchored and does not pass through.
- Keep the angle between every pair of adjacent fiber segments at most 60 degrees in the final configuration.
- Touch and grasp only the thick sheath with the gripper. Keep the completed passage valid through confirmation; holding is allowed, with no separate release or speed threshold.
- Seen from the ring's entry side, looking through the ring in the feed direction, the red fiber's root must end 30 deg counterclockwise from the top of the ring (about 11:00 on a clock face), within ±20 deg.
- Keep all final conditions satisfied together for at least 0.5 s of simulation time. If any final condition stops holding, the confirmation interval starts again. A brief qualifying pose is not enough; let the simulation advance through the full interval.

### Task failure conditions
- Any gripper touch of a fine fiber at any time ends the episode in failure, including a brief or zero-force touch.
- A fiber link falling more than 100 mm below the tabletop ends the episode in failure.

### Grader details (in addition to the requirements above)
- Robot safety (this benchmark's tooling): no gripper target goes lower than 15 mm below the tabletop; a commanded target that would bring any part of a gripper lower is stopped there, on the commanded path. The fingertips reach about 6 mm beyond the tool frame (TCP) along its approach axis when open and about 19 mm when closed (the Robotiq fingertips move forward as they close). Arm drive torques are capped (the effort limits in robot_spec), so an arm pushing against an obstacle stalls at that torque. An arm with any part more than 5 mm deep in a fixed object (a fixture, the table) is stopped where it is: its joint targets become its measured joints for that control step, so it stops pushing (reported in clipped[]).
- Forcing is recorded, not judged: an arm drive (not a gripper drive) at 95% of its torque cap or more while that arm's gripper, or a part it holds, presses on anything else with 20 N or more in total is forcing; 0.5 s of it on every physics tick is a forcing event in the evidence (`meta["safety"]`), shown to the reviewer when a success is reviewed. Forcing does not end the episode and does not change the grade by itself.
- Sizes: ring inner radius 13.5 mm, ring tube radius 4 mm; 16 fibers of radius 1.2 mm; sheath radius 12 mm.
- Each fiber is tracked at 11 points from tip to root. A point counts when it crosses the ring's plane less than 17.48 mm from the ring's axis, in tip-to-root order; a crossing out of order or outside that radius resets that point and every point behind it, and crossing back loses the credit.
- At the end every fiber point must be more than 5.2 mm past the ring's plane (tube + fiber radius) and the centre of the sheath's front face more than 16 mm past it (tube + sheath radius). The exit side is the side opposite to where the fibers start.
- The failure counts contact between a fine fiber and the gripper (fingers and hand, housing included); arm links touching a fiber do not count. Contact is sampled at all 4 solver substeps of every tick; if any tick since the start had incomplete contact coverage (a simulator-side condition), the episode can no longer succeed.
- Grasp regions (assisted grasping, see Grippers above; one sphere per graspable part): fiber_bundle: radius 25 mm. Both finger contacts must lie inside the part's sphere; a part touched elsewhere is grasped physically.
