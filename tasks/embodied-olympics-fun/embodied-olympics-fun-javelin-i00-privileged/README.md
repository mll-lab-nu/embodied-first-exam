## Task
Throw the javelin held by its cord in your right hand over the shoulder from behind the arc so that its head strikes the ground first, inside the sector, at least 20.0 m from the arc, then leave the runway behind the arc and stand. (Embodied Olympics `javelin`: one Unitree H1-2 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
20.0 m asks for a release near 18 m/s at 25 to 30 degrees with the javelin pointing close enough to its flight that it comes down head first; the H1-2 has no running policy and its arm motors give 40 N m at the shoulder. Nothing holds the 2.21 m javelin but the fingers round its cord: a whip of the arm pulls it out across them (the finger drives give way at their self-locking limits), and the hand must let go of it at the right moment, above the shoulder, on a body that has to stay on its feet behind the arc.

## Reference solution
None. No reference controller exists for this family yet: The cord lies across the palm of the H1-2's own right Inspire hand (its default forces, 20 N per fingertip, 30 N the thumb), held by contact and friction. A fast overhand swing pulls the cord out of the hand: its load back-drives the finger drives to their self-locking limits and pulls the cord out across the fingers at about 1.14 s (before the commanded opening at 1.18 s), at 12-13 m/s pointing down, at 1x and equally at 2x these forces (so no factor up to 2x helps); a swing searched by CMA-ES (about 60 generations) reaches 9.8 m head first (the mark is 20 m). Positive by a declared magic action: real arm raised above the shoulder holding the javelin, the hand opened; injected once, the javelin moved 0.12 m up and given 17 m/s at 35 deg: it lands head first 31.6 m from the arc; the real walk back off the runway and stand; the same state hash twice. Negatives: nop, a release below the shoulder, the hand opened at the start, the back turned, walking over the arc, leaving the runway while it flies, tail first, outside the sector, short, touched after the release, out in front of the arc's ends, a fall. Stress: random arm targets with the hand closed: at most 0.4 mm into the fingers. The clean release (TR 38.1(a), held with one hand, not slung or hurled): the release is the javelin's last contact with any part of the robot after the hold ends (then 0.02 s free), with at most 0.2 s of unheld contact on the right arm in between; after it, brushes by the right arm in the 0.2 s grace may change its velocity by at most 0.5 m/s in all (the contact impulses, summed, over its mass). A model run's 21.2 m throw (the hold ended at 5.3 m/s, then batted by the opening hand to 20.8 m/s) fails: release at its last continuous contact (7.078 s, 8.6 m/s), then 'struck after the release' (5.1 m/s by contact at 7.109 s). ## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in one fresh process (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success. The rules: The women's javelin (0.6 kg, 2.21 m long, its centre of gravity 0.92 m from the tip, a metal head of 0.28 m; its cord grip, 0.145 m long and 0.0287 m across, covers the centre of gravity) starts held at its cord across the palm of your right hand, a five-finger hand that holds only by contact and friction: the javelin can slip along the fingers or turn in the hand, and drop. It is held while the cord touches the hand's fingers and its thumb or palm (breaks of at most 0.02 s are ignored). The release is the javelin's last contact with any part of you after the hold has ended (however it ends: opened, slipped or dropped), once it has then touched nothing of you for 0.02 s; between the end of the hold and the release only your right arm and hand may touch it, for at most 0.2 s. Until the release the javelin may not touch your left arm or hand (the javelin is held with one hand). The runway is 4.0 m wide between the inner edges of its side lines and 30.0 m long; it ends at the arc, a white strip whose inner edge is a circle of 8.0 m radius round a point on the runway's centre line; short white lines run outwards from the arc's two ends at right angles to the side lines. The sector is bounded by the two lines from the arc's centre through the arc's ends (28.96 degrees between them). The throw: at the release the javelin's centre of gravity must be at least 0.0 m higher than your right shoulder joint. There is one release: after it the javelin may not be held again (the cord in the hand's fingers and thumb or palm once more) and no part of you may touch it, except that your right arm and its hand may still brush it during the first 0.2 s, if those touches change its velocity by at most 0.5 m/s in all (the sum of their contact impulses over its mass: the release must be clean). The throw is valid only if the head touches the ground before any other part of the javelin, at a point inside the sector lines (on a line is out). It is measured from that point to the inner edge of the arc along the line through the arc's centre. You succeed when a valid throw measures at least 20.0 m and you have then left the runway correctly and stood for 1.0 s. Leaving correctly: after the javelin has landed, your first touch of a side line or of the ground outside the runway is behind the arc's end lines, or you touch the ground 4.0 m or more behind the arc's ends. Standing: only your feet on the ground, the torso within about 20 degrees of upright, the pelvis slower than 0.35 m/s. You fail if, at any time before that, any part of you touches the arc, its end lines or the ground beyond them; if you touch a side line or the ground outside the runway (also behind its rear end) before the javelin has landed; if your chest is turned more than 135.0 degrees away from the throwing direction before the release (your back to the arc); if the javelin touches your left arm or hand before the release; if it is released lower than the shoulder; if after the end of the hold it stays on your right arm for more than 0.2 s or touches another part of you; if it is held again, or touched, after the release (but for the brush above); if it does not land head first, lands outside the sector or short of 20.0 m; if you fall (your pelvis lower than 0.5 m above the ground, or any part of you other than your feet on the ground); or after 60 s. Where you touch the ground is judged on the points of contact. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §4).
