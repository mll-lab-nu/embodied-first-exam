## Task
Make a layup: with the basketball pressed between your two palms take at most two steps towards the basket, jump, and let go of the ball in the air close to the ring so that it enters the basket from above. (Embodied Olympics `layup-realgrasp`: one Unitree H1-2 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
Two steps and a jump have to carry a 67 kg body almost two metres while the arms keep squeezing the ball through every footfall, and the release comes in the air, about a metre below a ring that is 0.21 m wider than the ball: the palms have to open so that the ball leaves them with the speed of the arms' own push, since a palm does not bounce it. The robot has a walking gait and nothing that jumps or lands; the built-in walking gait steps on the spot even at a zero command, so while the ball is in your hands its steps are counted like any others.

## Reference solution
None. No reference controller exists for this family yet: positive by injection (a real two-palm squeeze raised overhead in stand mode and carried through two real walking steps, robot and ball teleported together and given the same 2.6 m/s ghost take-off, the palms really opened at the apex (release judged by contact), a toss set once the ball is clear of both palms); negatives: a third step, the same foot twice, a release/slip from the floor (no squeeze: out in 0.03 s; one palm: 0.09 s), in the air without a take-off, too far, a miss, caught again, from below, held ball on the floor, held against the chest, a shuffle, a fall; grip columns do nothing. The H1-2 has no jump.

## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in two fresh processes (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success in both
and both end in the same state. The rules: The official basket: a rigid ring of 0.45 m inner diameter (0.018 m metal), its top edge 3.05 m above the floor, its centre 0.375 m in front of a backboard 1.8 m wide and 1.05 m high whose lower edge is 2.9 m above the floor. The ball is a size-7 basketball (0.239 m across, 0.62 kg, it keeps 0.85 of its speed in a bounce on the floor); it flies in air (drag and spin lift). Your hands have no fingers and no grip: the ball starts pressed between your two flat palms, and only the force of your arms and friction hold it; it can slip out. A hand is the palm pad and the wrist; a hand is on the ball while it touches the ball, gaps shorter than 0.03 s included; the ball is in your hands while at least one hand is on it, and you let go of it at the last touch of a hand after which no hand has been on it for 0.03 s. While the ball is in your hands it may touch no part of you but your hands, and it may not touch the floor. Travelling: while the ball is in your hands you may take at most 2 steps. A step is a foot touching the floor after it has been off it for at least 0.03 s; both feet landing within 0.03 s of each other are one step; the same foot may not touch down twice in a row, and a foot on the floor may not slide more than 0.2 m from where it stood or was put down (a shuffle is travelling). After that no foot may touch the floor again until you have let go of the ball. The release: at the moment you let go you must be in the air: nothing of you has touched anything but the ball for at least 0.05 s, after a take-off at which your pelvis was moving upwards at 0.5 m/s or more; and the ball's centre must be within 1.25 m (horizontally) of the ring's centre, the radius of the no-charge semi-circle painted under the basket. You succeed when, after that release, the ball's centre goes down through the ring's plane inside the ring and on down to one ball diameter below it (off the backboard or not). You fail if you travel; if the ball touches the floor or a part of you other than a hand while it is in your hands; if you let go of the ball (on purpose or because it slipped) with something of you on the floor, without a take-off, or further from the ring; if any part of you touches the ball more than 0.05 s after you let go of it (no catching it again); if the ball touches the floor before it is through the ring; if the ball's centre comes up through the ring from below; if you fall before the ball is through (a fall: your pelvis lower than 0.35 m above the floor, or any part of you other than your feet on the floor); or after 15 s. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §2.7).
