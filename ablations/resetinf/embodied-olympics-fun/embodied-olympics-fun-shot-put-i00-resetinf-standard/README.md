## Task
Put the shot that rests on your open right palm at your neck, from the shoulder with that hand alone, so that it lands inside the sector at least 6.0 m from the circle, then leave the circle from its rear half and stand. (Embodied Olympics `shot-put`; the robot as a service: its head cameras, joint sensors and IMUs, and the venue's fixed camera only.)

## Difficulty
The shot weighs 7.26 kg and rests on a flat palm without fingers: every move of the trunk or the arm that tilts the palm or accelerates the hand sideways rolls it off, and the H1-2's arm motors (40 N m at the shoulder, 18 N m at the elbow, soft servos) only just carry it, so the legs and the trunk have to drive the put while the palm stays under and behind the shot, inside a circle two steps wide. 6.0 m needs a release near 7 m/s at about 40 degrees. In standard mode the agent has no simulator and no state: it sees through the robot's cameras, acts through the client, and has 5 resets (only the last attempt is graded).

## Reference solution
None (as `embodied-olympics-fun-shot-put-i00-privileged`).

## Verification
The sim sidecar's referee judges the live attempt, and that is the verdict (`final.json`: `graded_by` live, `replays` 0;
no grading replay, owner 2026-10-17); the frozen start is checked on the live simulator (`instance_mismatch`:
infrastructure), and the video is rendered from the recorded attempt afterwards. `/tests/grade.py` reads
`/data/final.json`: pass iff the live attempt succeeded. The
rules: The men's shot (7.26 kg, 0.12 m across) starts resting on your open right palm beside your neck, leaning against the neck: you have taken your stance. Your hands have no fingers and no grip (the grip columns do nothing; nothing is grippable): only the palm's support, your neck and friction hold the shot, and it rolls or slides off when the palm does not carry it. The circle is 2.135 m across inside its rim; the ground outside is 0.02 m higher than its inside. At the front, a stop board 1.21 m long, 0.112 m wide and 0.1 m high follows the rim. The sector opens from the circle's centre, 34.92 degrees wide, symmetric about the direction you face at the start. Holding: the shot touches your right hand (the palm pad and the wrist); breaks of that contact shorter than 0.03 s are part of the hold. The release is the hand's last contact before a break of at least 0.03 s; at that moment of the break's end the shot must touch no part of you (a shot that leaves the hand but still rests on your trunk is a failure). The put (TR 33.1), judged on the shot's centre relative to your trunk (the line through your two shoulder joints, your trunk's up and forward directions) from the start to the release: the centre never lower than 0.0 m below the line of the shoulders and never more than 0.0 m behind it; nothing but your right hand and your trunk touches the shot; and at the release the shot must be moving away from your right shoulder joint with at least 0.5 of its speed relative to that joint (a push from the shoulder, not a throw or a sling). There is one release: after it nothing of you may touch the shot. The shot's first touch of the ground must be inside the sector lines (on a line is out); the put is measured from that point to the inside of the rim along the line through the circle's centre. You succeed when a valid put measures at least 6.0 m and you have then left the circle correctly and stood for 1.0 s. Leaving correctly: after the shot has landed, your first touch of the rim's top or of the ground outside the circle is behind the line through the circle's centre across the putting direction (the rear half). Standing: only your feet on the ground, the torso within about 20 degrees of upright, the pelvis slower than 0.35 m/s. You fail if any part of you touches the rim's top (its top inside edge included), the ground outside the circle or any part of the stop board other than its inner side (its top edge is not its inner side) before the shot has landed, or in the front half before you have left correctly; if the shot drops below or goes behind the line of the shoulders (falling off the palm included), is touched by anything but the right hand and the trunk, leaves the hand onto the trunk, or is not pushed at the release; if it lands outside the sector or short of 6.0 m; if any part of you touches it after the release; if you fall (your pelvis lower than 0.5 m above the circle's floor, or any part of you other than your feet on the ground); or after 60 s. Where you touch the ground is judged on the points of contact; a contact within 0.003 m of the rim's top, or that far outside the stop board's inner side, counts as on the top. The neck line of the stance runs from 0.08 m above the middle of the shoulder line to the chin, 0.2 m above and 0.09 m in front of it; at the start the shot's centre is within 0.22 m of it. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §4).
