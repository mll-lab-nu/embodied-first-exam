# Carry the wheel to the basket (Unitree G1, walking, Pink IK) — standard mode

The task of `embodiedswe-locomanip-wheel-carry-g1-loco-pink-ik-i00-privileged`, on the same frozen scene. You drive a Unitree G1 humanoid with three-finger hands, walking under EmbodiedSWE's frozen locomotion policy, its arms and waist under its Pink IK, as if it were real hardware: the simulator runs in a service you cannot open, and you get its client, one episode and no reset. The client's docstring is its manual (`/opt/tools/rcb_client.py`); `r.spec()` gives the numbers.

## The task
Two heavy-duty packing tables stand 3.0 m apart, their tops 0.69 m off the floor. On the near table a car steering wheel about 29 cm across lies flat, hub up. The far table carries an open plastic basket, its rim 0.78 m up and its interior about 70 x 46 cm — roomy enough to take the wheel lying flat. Nothing stands between the two tables but open floor.
Goal: pick the steering wheel up off the near table, carry it to the far table, and put it in the basket. The task is complete once the wheel is resting inside the basket, released and come to rest — not still held, not balanced on the rim.
The two tables are further apart than any arm reaches, so the wheel can only get there if the robot itself does.

Success, exactly (floor frame: origin at the robot's start spot, +y straight ahead toward the near table, +x to its right toward the far table, z up): (a) at some moment the steering wheel's origin, which starts 5.6 mm above the near table top with the wheel flat, rose above 0.744 m (5 cm above the 0.694 m table top). (b) At or after that moment, the origin was more than 2.70 m to the right (x > 2.7 m, 90% of the 3.0 m table-to-table offset). (c) At the end, the wheel's origin is inside the basket target zone: x between 3.40 and 3.85 m, y between 0.35 and 0.60 m (an inset within the basket's ~0.70 x 0.46 m interior), and height below 1.100 m (0.406 m above the far table top). Every component of its linear (m/s) and angular (rad/s) velocity is under 0.20. Not true at the start.

## This robot
- Action: `ee_pose` only, 32 values: the world poses of the left and right `wrist_yaw_link` (`x y z qw qx qy qz` each), 14 hand joint targets (rad), then the base command `vx vy wz hip_height` (`[0, 0, 0, 0.72]` stands still; the wrist poses are in the world frame, so move them with the robot while it walks). Rows are streamed at the 60 Hz control rate with your `dt`; `spec["action_types"]["ee_pose"]` has the layout, the controller and the joint limits. Coordinates are the simulator's world frame (metres, z up; `spec["world"]`).
- Cameras (RGB 640×480, depth on request): `wide`, `pick`, `place`, `overview` (static) and `head` (on the torso, where the real G1 carries its depth camera). One call returns at most 240 images (`max_frames_per_call`, PROTOCOL §3 "Images per call": cameras × the frames captured, the first, one every round(60 / `video_fps`) control steps and the last, a per-frame depth one more image; `video="none"` captures only the first and the last); a larger request is refused (400) before anything runs. A call that names no cameras gets `wide`, `head` at 3 frames per second, so every call up to `max_chunk_s` / `max_watch_s` fits.
- The episode ends after the first call at whose end the success condition holds (judged on the live simulator), when your time is up, on a simulator error, or after 400,000 control steps (1.9 h of simulated time, reason `step_guard`); it is graded as it stands then. Simulated time advances only inside your calls.
- The service answers one request at a time: while a long call runs, every other request waits, so a client-side timeout means "busy", not "crashed".
- Leave your scripts in `/app/output/` for the record.

## Time limit
You have 14400 seconds to complete this task. Do not cheat by using online solutions or hints specific to this task.

This is wall-clock time from the start of your session; the time you wait for the simulator service counts. When it is up you are stopped without warning and the episode is graded as it stands at that moment. The service's `time_left_s` counts the same limit from your first request to the service, so it never runs out before your time does: your real time left is `time_left_s` minus the time you spent before that request. Plan by it. Your session started when you received this task. `/run/rcb-limits/usage.json`, rewritten every few seconds, gives the time left on this limit (`time_left_s`) and its end (`deadline_utc`), counted from a little before your session started, so it never gives you more time than you have: read it (or run `date`) before you decide that time is short, never guess. Waiting counts too: a tool call that sends input to a process that keeps running (an interactive interpreter, a simulator loop, a server) can wait for the whole wait or yield time you give it even when the answer is printed at once, so give such calls a short wait and poll again if the output is not complete, or run one-shot scripts that exit when they are done. Installing packages is not cheating (`## External material` above).
