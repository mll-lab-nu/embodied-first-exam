## Task
Throw the javelin held by its cord in your right gripper over the shoulder from behind the arc so that its head strikes the ground first, inside the sector, at least 20.0 m from the arc, then leave the runway behind the arc and stand. (Embodied Olympics `javelin-realgrasp`: one Unitree H1-2 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
20.0 m asks for a release at about 14 m/s near 35 to 40 degrees with the javelin pointing along its flight, or it will not come down head first; the H1-2 has no running policy and its arm motors give 40 N m at the shoulder. Nothing holds the 2.65 m javelin but two jaws 34 mm wide on its cord: a whip of the arm turns it in them, and the jaws must let go of it at the right moment, above the shoulder, on a body that has to stay on its feet behind the arc.

## Reference solution
`solution/oracle.py` drives the robot through the task's own tooling, closed loop on the privileged state, and writes the trajectory (`solution/solve.sh`). Reference: javelin's standing throw re-searched by CMA-ES for a cord held only by the friction of the right Dex1 gripper (javelin's own technique twists it in the jaws and drops it in the wind-up): it slips out past the fingertips at 16.8 m/s, 32 degrees, head first at 30.96 m (the line is 20 m), then back behind the arc and standing; 13/13 under gain x0.94-1.06, release command +-5 ms and 1 cm start shifts, 5/10 under 0.02 rad key jitter. Referee test: an injected throw after a real sideways raise and jaw opening; negatives: held to the end, released below the shoulder (jaws opened arm down; a 3 N clamp sliding out), held again, the left hand on it, the back turned, over the arc, off the runway early, tail first, outside the sector, short, touched after the release, out in front, a fall Measured (make_oracles.py): success after 12.1 s of the 60 s limit, live and in a fresh replay, ending in the same state.

## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in two fresh processes (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success in both
and both end in the same state. The rules: The men's javelin (0.8 kg, 2.65 m long, its centre of gravity 0.98 m from the tip, a metal head of 0.29 m; its cord grip, 0.16 m long and 0.029 m across, covers the centre of gravity) starts held at its cord between the two jaws of your right hand, a two-jaw gripper that holds only by friction: the javelin can slip along or turn in the jaws, and drop. It is held while both jaws touch the cord (breaks of at most 0.02 s are ignored); the release is the end of the hold: the last moment both jaws touched the cord, however it ends (opened, slipped or dropped). Until the release the javelin may not touch your left arm or hand (the javelin is held with one hand). The runway is 4.0 m wide between the inner edges of its side lines and 30.0 m long; it ends at the arc, a white strip whose inner edge is a circle of 8.0 m radius round a point on the runway's centre line; short white lines run outwards from the arc's two ends at right angles to the side lines. The sector is bounded by the two lines from the arc's centre through the arc's ends (28.96 degrees between them). The throw: at the release the javelin's centre of gravity must be at least 0.0 m higher than your right shoulder joint. There is one release: after it the javelin may not be held again (both jaws on the cord once more) and no part of you may touch it, except that your right arm and its gripper may still touch it during the first 0.2 s (the shaft sliding out through the jaws). The throw is valid only if the head touches the ground before any other part of the javelin, at a point inside the sector lines (on a line is out). It is measured from that point to the inner edge of the arc along the line through the arc's centre. You succeed when a valid throw measures at least 20.0 m and you have then left the runway correctly and stood for 1.0 s. Leaving correctly: after the javelin has landed, your first touch of a side line or of the ground outside the runway is behind the arc's end lines, or you touch the ground 4.0 m or more behind the arc's ends. Standing: only your feet on the ground, the torso within about 20 degrees of upright, the pelvis slower than 0.35 m/s. You fail if, at any time before that, any part of you touches the arc, its end lines or the ground beyond them; if you touch a side line or the ground outside the runway (also behind its rear end) before the javelin has landed; if your chest is turned more than 135.0 degrees away from the throwing direction before the release (your back to the arc); if the javelin touches your left arm or hand before the release; if it is released lower than the shoulder; if it is held again, or touched, after the release (but for the grace above); if it does not land head first, lands outside the sector or short of 20.0 m; if you fall (your pelvis lower than 0.5 m above the ground, or any part of you other than your feet on the ground); or after 60 s. Where you touch the ground is judged on the points of contact.
