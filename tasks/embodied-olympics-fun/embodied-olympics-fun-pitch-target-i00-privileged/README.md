## Task
Pitch a strike: from the pitcher's plate on the mound, with a foot on the plate, throw the baseball held in your right hand through the strike zone over home plate, on the fly. (Embodied Olympics `pitch-target`: one Unitree H1-2 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
From 18.44 m the ball needs more than 13 m/s even on a high arc, and the zone is 0.43 m wide: a release direction right to about one degree. The H1-2 has no throwing motion; the speed has to come from the trunk and the whole arm while a foot stays on the plate and the body on the mound, and fingers that open no faster than 260 deg/s have to let the ball go at the right hundredth of a second.

## Reference solution
None. No reference controller exists for this family yet: The H1-2's own right Inspire hand at its default forces (20 N per fingertip, 30 N the thumb) holds the baseball against its palm by contact and friction (no weld). Positive by a declared magic action: real stance on the mound with both feet across the pitcher's plate and the hand opening; injected once, 10 ms after it starts to open, the ball moved 0.1 m forwards and up and given 22 m/s at 9.5 deg: released on the plate, it crosses the zone at 0.91 m height, 0.02 m off centre, at 20 m/s (strike); the same state hash twice; a 14.5 m/s lob through the zone is also a strike. Negatives: nop (pitch timer, ball still in the hand), release with no foot on the plate (behind it, in front of it), a bounce, high, wide, the hand opened (the ball drops to the mound), the ball touched after the release, a fall before the call. Stress: random arm targets with the hand closed: the ball drops out, never through the fingers (at most 1.9 mm into them). The clean release (the same rule as javelin): brushes by the right arm in the 0.2 s grace after the release may change the ball's velocity by at most 0.5 m/s in all; an injected blow on the palm (1.8 m/s) fails as 'struck after the release'. An agent's own-motor strike from a model run succeeds under it. Every rule above is judged in the family's own 1 ms substep hook..

## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in one fresh process (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success. The rules: The baseball (0.073 m across, 0.145 kg, it flies in air with drag and spin lift) starts held in your right hand, a five-finger hand that holds only by contact and friction: the ball can slip or roll out and drop. You stand on the pitcher's plate (a slab 0.61 m wide and 0.152 m deep, level with the mound's top) on a mound 5.486 m across whose top is 0.254 m above home plate; in front of the plate the mound falls 1 in 12. The front edge of the pitcher's plate is 18.44 m from the back point of home plate; home plate is 0.432 m wide and as deep, so its front edge is that much nearer. The strike zone is the rectangle above home plate's front edge, 0.432 m wide and from 0.46 m to 1.07 m above home plate. The pitch: the release is the moment the ball last touched you, once it has then touched nothing of you for 0.02 s (until then it is still in your hand, wherever it rests on you). There is one release: within 15.0 s of the start, and at that moment at least one of your feet is in contact with the pitcher's plate (it touches the mound and part of its sole is over the plate). After the release no part of you may touch the ball and your right hand may not hold it again (its fingers and its thumb or palm on it), except that your right arm and its hand may still brush it during the first 0.2 s, if those touches change its velocity by at most 0.5 m/s in all (the sum of their contact impulses over its mass: the release must be clean). You succeed when the ball's centre then crosses the plane of the zone inside the zone, before the ball has touched the ground. You fail if no foot is on the plate at the release; if the ball is not released in time; if it touches the ground (the mound included) at any time before home plate, also before the release; if it crosses the plane outside the zone (a ball); if it is held again or touched after the release (but for the grace above); if you fall before the call (your pelvis lower than 0.75 m above home plate's level, or any part of you other than your feet on the ground or the mound); or after 20 s. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §4).
