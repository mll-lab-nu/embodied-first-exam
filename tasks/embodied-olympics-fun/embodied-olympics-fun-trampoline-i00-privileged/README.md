## Task
On the trampoline, bounce as you like, then do one tuck jump, land on both feet, stop the bounce (one more straight out-bounce at most) and stand still. (Embodied Olympics `trampoline`: one Unitree G1 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
The robot must time its leg push with a bed that drops and recoils under it to gain height, tuck and open again in the air, land on both feet, and then kill the bounce of a springy bed with its legs until it stands still.

## Reference solution
`solution/oracle.py` drives the robot through the task's own tooling, closed loop on the privileged state, and writes the trajectory (`solution/solve.sh`). Reference: a scripted G1 tuck jump (a balanced crouch, a lowcmd push on the sprung bed, the tuck held mid-flight, a squat landing that sinks as the bed recoils, one straight out-bounce, an ankle-strategy stand-up, 3 s still), chosen from 12 settings: 0.457 s of flight, a centre-of-mass rise of 0.32 m. The bed passes the FIG IV TRA 1 impact test in simulation (60 kg sphere from 4.00 m: deflection 0.988 m, in 940-1010 mm; rebound 3.486 m, in 3410-3660 mm). Negatives: nop, a tuck without a jump, pike first, a bent out-bounce, a second flight, a fall, an injected roll spin, an injected push onto the padding. Measured: success after 7.55 s of the 30 s limit, live and in a fresh replay, ending in the same state.

## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in one fresh process (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success. The rules: You stand at the centre of the trampoline's bed, 1.155 m above the floor, facing +x along its length. A flight is a time of at least 0.1 s in which nothing of you touches anything; your centre of mass's height when you stand at the start is the reference height. Straight bounces first are free. The tuck jump is your first flight in which your body is not straight: at some moment of its middle half (from a quarter to three quarters of the flight's time) each leg must be tucked: the angle at the hip between your shoulder and your knee, and the angle at the knee between your hip and your ankle, both 135.0 degrees or less (180 is straight), held for at least 0.05 s without a break, and it must be a jump: in it your centre of mass rises at least 0.1 m above the reference height (a tuck without that height fails). A flight before it in which a hip or a knee closes to 135.0 degrees or less in the middle half without the tuck is another element and fails. Every landing must be on the bed, on your feet only, both feet within 0.1 s of each other. After the tuck jump's landing you may make one more flight, an out-bounce, which must be straight (hips and knees more open than 135.0 degrees through its middle half); a second flight fails. Then stop: stand still for 3.0 s without a break: only your feet on the bed, your torso within about 20 degrees of vertical, your pelvis slower than 0.35 m/s, your ankles at most 0.28 m apart. You fail on a landing with any other part of you on the bed, on touching anything other than the bed (the padding over the frame and springs, the end platforms, the floor), or after 30 s. Success: the tuck jump, its landing and the stop, in that order.
