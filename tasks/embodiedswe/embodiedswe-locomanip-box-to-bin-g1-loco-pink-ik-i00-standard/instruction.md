# Carry the box from the shelf to the bin (Unitree G1, walking, Pink IK) — standard mode

The task of `embodiedswe-locomanip-box-to-bin-g1-loco-pink-ik-i00-privileged`, on the same frozen scene. You drive a Unitree G1 humanoid with three-finger hands, walking under EmbodiedSWE's frozen locomotion policy, its arms and waist under its Pink IK, as if it were real hardware: the simulator runs in a service you cannot open, and you get its client, one episode and no reset. The client's docstring is its manual (`/opt/tools/rcb_client.py`); `r.spec()` gives the numbers.

## The task
A steel shelving unit stands with its face 0.46 m ahead of the start pose, boards at 0.41, 0.76 and 1.49 m. On the middle board sits a brown cardboard box, 20 cm on a side and about 0.1 kg, its center 0.86 m up, nearly flush with the shelf's front edge and with 0.69 m of clear air above it. To the right, about 1.4 m away, a low table (top 0.53 m) carries an open blue sorting bin: interior about 48 x 72 cm around (1.81, -0.24), floor at 0.53 m, walls just 0.05 m tall — a shallow tray the box drops into, not a deep basket.
Goal: pick the box up off the shelf, carry it to the table, and leave it resting inside the bin. The task is complete once the box sits fully inside the bin's walls, released and come to rest — not still held, not straddling the rim.
The bin is further away than any arm reaches, so the box can only get there if the robot itself does — and the table stands off the shelf's axis, so the route turns.

Success, exactly (floor frame: origin at the robot's start spot, +y straight ahead toward the shelf, +x to its right, z up): (a) at some moment the brown box was taken off the shelf. Either its centre rose above 0.913 m (5 cm above its 0.863 m resting height), or it was held above 0.35 m inside the window in front of the shelf face (y between 0.063 and 0.413 m, |x| < 0.50 m). (b) At or after that moment, its centre came within 0.1986 m horizontally of the bin centre (1.8072, -0.245), i.e. it closed more than 90% of the 1.986 m start-to-bin distance. (c) At the end, the box centre has x between 1.6672 and 1.9472 m, y between -0.505 and 0.015 m, and height below 0.6666 m (0.136 m above the 0.5306 m table top, which excludes a box lying across the rim). Every component of its linear (m/s) and angular (rad/s) velocity is under 0.20. Not true at the start.

## This robot
- Action: `ee_pose` only, 32 values: the world poses of the left and right `wrist_yaw_link` (`x y z qw qx qy qz` each), 14 hand joint targets (rad), then the base command `vx vy wz hip_height` (`[0, 0, 0, 0.72]` stands still; the wrist poses are in the world frame, so move them with the robot while it walks). Rows are streamed at the 60 Hz control rate with your `dt`; `spec["action_types"]["ee_pose"]` has the layout, the controller and the joint limits. Coordinates are the simulator's world frame (metres, z up; `spec["world"]`).
- Cameras (RGB 640×480, depth on request): `wide`, `pick`, `place`, `overview` (static) and `head` (on the torso, where the real G1 carries its depth camera). One call returns at most 240 images (`max_frames_per_call`, PROTOCOL §3 "Images per call": cameras × the frames captured, the first, one every round(60 / `video_fps`) control steps and the last, a per-frame depth one more image; `video="none"` captures only the first and the last); a larger request is refused (400) before anything runs. A call that names no cameras gets `wide`, `head` at 3 frames per second, so every call up to `max_chunk_s` / `max_watch_s` fits.
- The episode ends after the first call at whose end the success condition holds (judged on the live simulator), when your time is up, on a simulator error, or after 400,000 control steps (1.9 h of simulated time, reason `step_guard`); it is graded as it stands then. Simulated time advances only inside your calls.
- The service answers one request at a time: while a long call runs, every other request waits, so a client-side timeout means "busy", not "crashed".
- Leave your scripts in `/app/output/` for the record.

The service's `time_left_s` counts the same limit from your first request to the service, so it never runs out before your time does: your real time left is `time_left_s` minus the time you spent before that request.
