# DexHand · Serve a pancake from a frying pan with a spatula (standard)

## Task
Serve the pancake from the frying pan onto the plate with the kitchen turner (a spatula).

## Goal
Success: in this order, the pancake rides lifted and roughly level on the turner's blade, touching nothing else, for a
moment; it is carried over the plate on the blade; then it lies flat in the middle of the plate, touching only the
plate, with the turner drawn back from it, and is still for a second.

Any of these ends the episode at once as a failure, which later actions cannot undo:
- The pancake touches the counter top or the floor, falls freely or drops below the counter top.
- Any part of the robot touches the pancake.
- The turner touches the floor, falls out of the hand or drops below the counter top.
- The pan or the plate is moved or tipped.
- The pancake, the pan, the plate or the turner leaves the work area.
- The physics becomes unstable.

The steps before the last count as they happen. Success is judged on how things stand when the episode ends. It ends
when you call finish (Limits) or when your session ends. The robot never tells you whether you have succeeded.

Rules:
- **No cheating.** Do not search for, download or use any existing solution (published or reference solutions, the
  benchmark's demonstrations, trained policies); trials are reviewed.
- Episodes: 20 resets (`spec()["budget"]["reset"]`), each a new episode from the same starting state; within an episode
  what you knock over stays knocked over until you reset. Only the last episode is graded: the episode you end with
  finish (Limits; no reset after it), or else the one running when your session ends.
- You get only the cameras and the robot's own sensor readings: no object poses, no scene layout, no score, no success
  signal.

Only the robot's actuator targets act on the scene: no object teleports, grasp welds, scripted object motion or applied
forces.

## Robot Interface
A fixed Tianji Marvin robot with two 7-joint arms and a dexterous hand on each wrist (the right arm and hand do the
task; the left arm stays parked), at a counter (DexHand, MuJoCo). The robot runs as a service you reach through its
client or its MCP tools, as if it were real hardware.

### Client
`/opt/tools/rcb_client.py`:
```python
from rcb_client import Robot
r = Robot()
spec = r.spec()
spec["hand"]                   # "sharpa" or "wuji"
obs = r.get_obs(cameras=["overview"], depth=True)
obs.last["overview"]           # RGB uint8 (480, 640, 3)
obs.last_depth["overview"]     # z-depth in metres
obs.meta["proprio"]            # named robot measurements
q = obs.group("joint_pos", now=True)
res = r.submit("joint_position", rows, dt=0.02, interpolate="hold", video="none")
r.fk(q)                       # robot-link forward kinematics; no physics is stepped
r.status()
res = r._post("/reset", {"cameras": ["overview"]}, [], 900)   # POST /reset (this client has no reset())
```
Requests can return RGB frames or mp4, with depth `none`, `last` or `frames`. Each call returns at most 1,000 camera
images, counting per-frame depth; reduce cameras, fps or duration for larger chunks. `hold(s)` holds the current targets
and records those steps. `get_obs()` only observes the current state. Forward kinematics uses only the robot's own
links; no IK primitive is supplied. If the service connection fails, call `r.wait_ready()` and inspect `r.status()`
before continuing. Crash recovery resumes the same journaled episode, without a reset, and the wall-clock budget keeps
running.

Resets: `r._post("/reset", ...)` (`POST /reset`) starts a new episode from the same starting state, abandoning the
running one or after one has ended; it takes no simulated time and returns the start's images and proprioception. Once
an episode has ended, `submit` raises `EpisodeOver` until you reset. `meta["episode"]` and `meta["resets_left"]` count
the episodes; `meta["control_steps"]` is the current episode's, `meta["control_steps_total"]` all of them.

### MCP tools
- The same service is also an MCP server, `efe-robot` (in your tool list), with one tool per endpoint: `spec`,
  `observe`, `submit`, `reset`, `kinematics`, `finish`. A tool takes its endpoint's request fields (any other field in
  `fields`) and returns the service's reply `meta` unchanged; images, arrays and video are written as files under
  `/app/obs/` (`.png`, `.npy`, `.mp4`) and the result lists them. Long action chunks can be passed as a file
  (`actions_file`).
- `observe` returns at once with what is already recorded: the current frame, or the last `last_s` seconds. Time passes
  only through `submit`, which answers when its rows have run; a `submit` with `interpolate` "hold", `seconds` and no
  rows lets time pass with the robot holding still.
- A call still running after 45 s returns `pending` with an `operation` id; `observe(operation=id)` collects its reply.
- The client and the tools drive the same robot in the same episode; use either, or both.

### Actions
`rows` is `(T, D)` in `spec["action_types"]["joint_position"]["layout"]` order, radians: columns 0-6 the left arm
`SHOULDER_PITCH_L_J1` ... `WRIST_ROLL_L_J7`, 7-13 the right arm `..._R_J1` ... `..._R_J7`, then the left hand's and the
right hand's finger targets. `D` is 58 for `sharpa` (22 Sharpa Wave finger joints per hand) and 54 for `wuji` (20 Wuji
Hand 2.2 finger joints per hand). Actuator targets are clipped to the published joint limits and clipping is reported.
`dt` must be a multiple of 0.02 s, between 0.02 and 0.5 s; a chunk covers at most 10 s. Hold interpolation holds each
row for `dt`; linear interpolation starts from the previous target. The robot has no scalar gripper opening.

Each row starts with the 14 Marvin arm joint targets (left arm J1-J7, then right arm J1-J7), followed by the left hand's
finger targets and then the right hand's (22 per hand for `sharpa`, 20 per hand for `wuji`); the left arm is parked and
only the right arm/hand perform the task.

### Observations
You receive camera RGB, optional calibrated depth, and the robot's own joint positions, velocities, actuator efforts,
wrist pose and current targets. Quaternions in measured poses use WXYZ; world z is up and the counter top is at z = 0.85
m. Cameras are overview, closeup and side, at 640 x 480.

### Hands
Two hand models: `sharpa` (Sharpa Wave, 22 finger actuators; the default) and `wuji` (Wuji Hand 2.2, 20 finger
actuators); the task is the same for both, and one hand is used per episode. The service runs the hand that
`DEXHAND_HAND` names on the sim service (default `sharpa`); you cannot switch it during an episode. The hand in use is
`spec()["hand"]`; `spec()["action_types"]["joint_position"]` gives the row width (`58` for `sharpa`, `54` for `wuji`)
and the exact order.

### Robot
The robot is a fixed Tianji Marvin stand at the world origin facing +x, with two 7-joint arms and a dexterous hand on
each wrist; the right arm and hand do the task and the left arm stays parked beside the torso. Every arm and finger
joint is a position servo in the physics engine under native force bounds; arm servos have stiffness 700 N m/rad,
damping 48 N m s/rad and no gravity compensation (a held target sags by about 1 cm at the hand under load unless your
targets account for it). The turner is held only by finger and palm contact, and the pancake moves only through contact
with the turner, the pan and the plate. The controller runs at 50 Hz, with twenty 1 ms physics substeps per control.
World frame: metres and radians, +z up, +x forward from the robot stand, +y to the robot's left.

### Scene
A 22 cm non-stick frying pan (0.90 kg, free on the counter) holds one cooked pancake (0.06 kg, about 140 mm across and
9.5 mm thick); an empty ceramic plate (0.48 kg, free, rim radius 0.128 m) stands beside it on an oak counter whose top
is at z = 0.85 m; the pan rests centred at (0.50, 0.10) and the plate at (0.76, 0.18). The OXO flexible kitchen turner
(0.15 kg) rests on the counter in front of the plate, at about (0.76, -0.30, 0.878) m with its handle toward -y (handle
end and blade base on the counter, the handle 16 to 30 mm above it). Friction is high for the hand on the turner (1.2)
and for the pancake on the plate (0.60), lower for the pancake on the pan (0.45) and low for the pancake and the pan on
the blade (0.20); objects on the counter have 0.7.

The episode starts with both hands open and the turner resting on the counter; holding the start targets keeps that
pose.

## Limits
- Time: 3600 s of wall-clock time from the start of your session; everything counts (waiting for the service too). At
  the end you are stopped without warning and the episode is graded as it stands. Time left:
  `/run/rcb-limits/usage.json` (`time_left_s`, `deadline_utc`); the service's `time_left_s` (in every reply) starts at
  your first request, so the file is the one to trust. Calls into a long-running process can block for their whole wait
  time: use short waits or one-shot scripts.
- Tokens: no separate limit.
- Network: your model's API and the package registries (PyPI, Debian / Ubuntu archives) only. You may install packages
  and read anything in your container, the simulator's and the benchmark's source included.
- Resources: 2 CPUs, 4 GiB memory, 1024 processes and threads, 10 GiB disk (new files under `/app`, `/tmp`, `/root`), no
  GPU; the simulator runs in its own container and does not count. Over a cap a process can be killed (the CPU cap only
  slows you down); usage: `/run/rcb-limits/usage.json`.
- Resets: at most 20 (`spec()["budget"]["reset"]`), each a new episode from the same starting state.
- Steps: at most 3,000 control steps (60 s of simulated time) per episode; an episode that reaches them ends there and
  is graded as it stands.
- Finish: when you are done, call finish: `POST /finish` with an empty JSON body to the service, at the client's base
  URL (`http://sim:8000` or `http://localhost:8000`; in Python `requests.post(r.base + "/finish", json={})` with your
  client `r`). It ends the episode, and every call after it is refused. Reaching the goal changes no reply and does not
  end the episode; if you never call finish, the end of your session ends it.
