## Task
Robot singles badminton on a regulation court in real time (slowed down): a Franka Panda arm with a racket on an x-y sliding base, a point-mass shuttle with high quadratic drag (terminal speed 6.8 m/s) and no bounce, a racket contact law; first to 5 points, rally scoring; the players' robots serve (the rally winner serves next): the server tosses the shuttle (from rest or rising up to 0.5 m) at a point it chooses and hits it underhand, below 1.15 m, into the diagonal service court; format match-wallclock: you against a scripted computer opponent (levels 1-2, `game spec` match.opponent_level), format duel-wallclock: two players; format `match-wallclock` (seats A),
action level `high`, program play. Generated by `tools/generate.py` from
`sports/badminton/sport.toml` (RoboOlympics task `badminton-match-wallclock-program-standard`, exported with `--upstream`: tools/upstream.toml); do not edit by hand.

Deviations from the rulebook:

- D1 (7): one short game: first to 5 points, from 4-4 a two-point lead is needed, at most 7 (6-6: the next point wins) instead of best of three games to 21 (cap 30); the match also ends after 300 hits (both sides) or 40 points played (lets included): the player ahead wins, at equal points the winner of the last point
- D2 (9.1.5-9.1.8): the robot has no free hand to hold and drop the shuttle: the server tosses it with the command toss {pos: [x, y, z], height: H} (the common racket-sport toss, docs/conventions/racket_serve_zh.md): when the command takes effect the shuttle appears at that point (0.5-1.8 m high, within 1.0 m horizontally of the server's base, on its half) and is released from rest (H = 0) or projected straight up to rise H (at most 0.5 m; the Laws do not forbid a small toss); until then it is held beside the server. The toss has no spin (a point mass has none, Law 9.1.5). Judged: the server's racket must hit it (a miss is a fault, Law 9.1.8), with the shuttle below 1.15 m at the hit (Law 9.1.6, judged at the shuttle's point: it has no size) and leaving the racket upwards (Law 9.1). Not judged: the racket hitting the base of the shuttle first and moving forwards throughout the service (Laws 9.1.5, 9.1.7)
- D3 (13.3-13.4): the shuttle is a point mass: a crossing of the net plane below the net top (between the posts) is a fault of the hitter; touching the net or reaching over it is not judged; a racket meets the shuttle only on its own half and only when it is that player's turn (no double hits)
- D4 (9.1.3-9.1.4): before every serve the referee puts both robots at rest in the ready pose at a fixed spot in their service courts (diagonally opposite: the right courts when the server's score is even, Law 10.1): x = +0.3 (right court) or -0.3 (left court), y = -4.0 in each robot's frame; the spot is the robot's home for the point (where its base returns between strokes). Standing still (Law 9.1.4) is judged on the base: a base more than 5 cm from its spot before the serve is delivered (hit, or faulted) is that player's fault; both at once: a let (Law 14.2.2). The base moves within |x| <= 3.25, -7.5 <= y <= -0.5 at up to 6 m/s and 20 m/s^2
- D5 (-): the last resort of the no-draw rule (equal points at the match limit and no point won) is not from the rulebook: the player who received first wins (format match-wallclock: the opponent)
- D6 (9.1.1, 16.6-16.7): undue delay has a fixed limit: the server must toss the shuttle within 3 s after the receiver is ready (1 s after play resumes, Law 9.4), else it is a service fault (the receiver's point); a player's third such fault disqualifies it (it loses the match) instead of the umpire's warning and the referee's discretion (Laws 16.7.1-16.7.2)
- D7 (6): no toss: format duel-wallclock: the runner plays both orders of the first server (match.first_server); format match-wallclock: you serve the first point

## Difficulty
Frozen instance (no run-time variables): seed 101 (it places the world and draws the execution scatter), opponent level 1, slowdown S = 10 (the world runs at 1/S of real speed). The RoboOlympics task `badminton-match-wallclock-program-standard` sets these at run time; this export fixes one official configuration, in which the reference player wins.

## Reference solution
`solution/solve.sh` runs the sport's reference player (`solution/builtin_players.py`) through the core's client
(`solution/ro_client.py`): camera images and `/spec` only.

## Verification
The `sim` service (the v0.2 core, `environment/sim/core/`) records the match (`episode.jsonl`) and a fresh process
replays it (state hash); the verifier (`tests/grade.py` = `core/grading.py`, no network) writes the common reward
fields (`success`, `valid_trial`, `deterministic`, `<seat>_won`, ...) and the sport's fields (`tests/sport_grade.py`).
