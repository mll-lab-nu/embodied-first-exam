# HumanCLAW · sit on the bed, i115 (standard)

## Task
Sit down on any bed in the house, and stop while you are still sitting on it.

## Goal
Success: when you stop, you are sitting on a bed. Your stop (action 1) declares that you are done and ends the episode;
`/finish` is the same stop, and so is the end of your session. Only a stop can succeed: if your 100th action is not a
stop, the episode fails there.

When you are done, send `/finish` to end the episode and get it graded. Only the state at that point counts.

Rules:
- **No cheating.** Do not search for, download or use any existing solution (published or reference solutions, the
  benchmark's demonstrations, trained policies); trials are reviewed.
- One episode, no reset: what you knock over stays knocked over.
- You see only what the head camera sees and feel how many actions are used and left and how far your pelvis is above
  (or below) its starting height; not where you are, not where the target is, not a map, no success signal.

## Robot Interface
A simulated adult human body in a furnished house (HumanCLAW: Habitat-Sim with Half-Physics, an HSSD scene); the body
moves only by motion skills: walk, turn, side step, step back, steps up or down, sit and stop. The robot runs as a
service you reach through its client or its MCP tools, as if it were real hardware: one live episode.

`POST /finish` (MCP tool `finish`): ends the episode; it is graded right away.

### Client
`/opt/tools/rcb_client.py`, protocol `eai-standard/2.1`. Its docstring is the full reference
(`python -c "import rcb_client; help(rcb_client)"`).
```python
from rcb_client import Robot
r = Robot()                                  # connects and waits until the scene is ready
r.spec()                                     # the body's facts, the action table, the camera
res = r.get_obs()                            # res.last["ego"]: the head camera's image; res.meta["proprio"]["now"]
res = r.submit("skill", [[2, 30.0], [0, 0.4]], video="none")   # turn left 30 degrees, then walk 0.4 m
res.last["ego"]                              # what the head camera sees after the last action
```
Time moves only inside calls. Each action is one 0.5 s motion; between calls the body waits while you think. `get_obs()`
is free; `hold(s)` lets s seconds pass with the body standing still (no action used). One call returns at most 3000
images.

### MCP tools
- The same service is also an MCP server, `efe-robot` (in your tool list), with one tool per endpoint: `spec`,
  `observe`, `submit`, `finish`. A tool takes its endpoint's request fields (any other field in `fields`) and returns
  the service's reply `meta` unchanged; images, arrays and video are written as files under `/app/obs/` (`.png`, `.npy`,
  `.mp4`) and the result lists them. Long action chunks can be passed as a file (`actions_file`).
- `observe` returns at once with what is already recorded: the current frame, or the last `last_s` seconds. Time passes
  only through `submit`, which answers when its rows have run; a `submit` with `interpolate` "hold", `seconds` and no
  rows lets time pass with the robot holding still.
- A call still running after 45 s returns `pending` with an `operation` id; `observe(operation=id)` collects its reply.
- The client and the tools drive the same robot in the same episode; use either, or both.

### Actions
Actions go as `submit("skill", A)`, rows `[action_id, value]`:

| id | action | value |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | walk forward | stride in m: `0.2`, `0.4` or `0.6` (nothing else) |
| 1 | stop | `0`. Ends the episode: you declare you are done. Only a stop can succeed. `r.finish()` is the same stop |
| 2 | turn in place | signed degrees, + left (counter-clockwise seen from above), - right; \|value\| clamped to [10, 120]; 0 is refused |
| 3 | climb up one step | `0` |
| 4 | sit down | target pelvis height in m, clamped to [0.15, 0.85] |
| 5 | step back | m, clamped to [0.10, 0.60] |
| 6 | side step | signed m, + left, - right; \|value\| clamped to [0.10, 0.50]; 0 is refused |
| 7 | walk down one step | `0` |

Each action except stop is one 0.5 s motion of the whole body (15 frames at 30 Hz), generated by HumanCLAW's motion
model and played out under physics: the body ends up close to, not exactly at, what was asked, and it can bump into
furniture (which can move).

A row that is invalid (an unknown id, a walk stride that is not 0.2 / 0.4 / 0.6, a turn of 0, a value on stop) refuses
the whole call (`ValueError`) and nothing runs; a clamped value is reported in `meta["clipped"]`. `dt` must be 0.5 s
(one row = one skill).

### Observations
- The head camera (`ego`): RGB, 448×448, 90° field of view; `res.last["ego"]` after the last action.
- Proprioception (`res.meta["proprio"]["now"]`): how many actions are used and left and how far your pelvis is above (or
  below) its starting height.

## Limits
- Time: 3600 s of wall-clock time from the start of your session; everything counts (waiting for the service too). At
  the end you are stopped without warning. Time left: `/run/rcb-limits/usage.json` (`time_left_s`, `deadline_utc`); the
  service's `time_left_s` (in every reply) starts at your first request, so the file is the one to trust. Calls into a
  long-running process can block for their whole wait time: use short waits or one-shot scripts.
- Tokens: no separate limit.
- Network: your model's API and the package registries (PyPI, Debian / Ubuntu archives) only. You may install packages
  and read anything in your container, the simulator's and the benchmark's source included.
- Resources: 4 CPUs, 3 GiB memory, 1024 processes and threads, 10 GiB disk (new files under `/app`, `/tmp`, `/root`), no
  GPU; the simulator runs in its own container and does not count. Over a cap a process can be killed (the CPU cap only
  slows you down); usage: `/run/rcb-limits/usage.json`.
- Resets: none: one episode.
- Actions: at most 100 per episode, the stop included.
- Finish: if you never send `/finish`, the episode is graded when the step limit is reached or your session ends.
