## Task
Serve: from behind your baseline, release the tennis ball from your left gripper, strike it with the racket held in your right gripper before it reaches the ground, and send it over the net into the service box diagonally opposite. (Embodied Olympics `tennis-serve-realgrasp`; the robot as a service: its head cameras, joint sensors and IMUs, and the venue's fixed camera only.)

## Difficulty
Nothing is fixed to the hands: the swing that gives a ball well over 10 m/s through a free 0.30 kg racket must not twist it out of two jaws that hold it by friction, the left gripper has to let go of the ball cleanly while the arm rises, and the strings must meet the falling ball within a few tenths of a second, aimed into a box 6.4 m deep beyond a net 11.9 m away. All of it on a body that stands without a balance controller and may not move its feet. In standard mode the agent has no simulator and no state: it sees through the robot's cameras, acts through the client, and has 50 resets (only the last attempt is graded).

## Verification
The sim sidecar's referee judges the live attempt, and that is the verdict (`final.json`: `graded_by` live, `replays` 0;
no grading replay, owner 2026-10-17, board 0151); the frozen start is checked on the live simulator (`instance_mismatch`:
infrastructure), and the video is rendered from the recorded attempt afterwards. `/tests/grade.py` reads
`/data/final.json`: pass iff the live attempt succeeded. The
rules: The court is the ITF singles court: 23.77 m long and 8.23 m wide, measured to the outside of the lines, with a service line 6.4 m from the net on each side and a centre service line that divides the space between the net and each service line into two service boxes. The net stands across the middle, 0.914 m high at its centre and rising in a straight line to 1.07 m at the posts, which stand 0.914 m outside the sidelines. The ball is the ITF ball (radius 0.0335 m, 0.0577 kg, with air drag and spin lift). Both your hands are two-jaw grippers that hold things only by the friction of their jaws: the ball starts held in your left gripper, the racket (0.685 m long, 0.30 kg, its balance 0.33 m from the butt, strung surface 0.33 m by 0.25 m, an octagonal handle 33.5 mm across) starts held by its handle in your right gripper; either can slip or fall. The racket is held while both jaws of your right gripper touch its handle; it must never go unheld for more than 0.05 s, from the start to the end of the attempt. You serve from the right of the centre mark into the far service box on the left of the centre line. In order: (1) the toss: the ball is released when it has touched nothing at all for 0.1 s (a ball resting on something is not released; the racket touching the ball before the release is a fault); at the end of those 0.1 s you must stand at rest, both feet on the ground behind the baseline, between the extension of the centre mark and the extension of the right sideline, neither foot having left the ground or moved more than 0.02 m in the last 0.5 s (so not before 0.5 s after the start); (2) the strike: the racket hits the ball before it reaches the ground (any part of the racket counts: strings or frame), the ball having touched nothing of you for the last 0.1 s and moving at most 5.0 m/s when it reaches the racket (a toss, not a throw), and the hit speeds the ball up (0.05 s after its last contact with the racket it is faster than it was when it reached the racket); (3) the ball's centre passes the net's plane between the posts above the net's top edge; (4) its first contact with the ground is inside that service box, the lines included (the point under the ball's centre decides; ground contacts less than 0.02 s apart are one): that is the success. From the release to the strike your feet may not touch the baseline or the court, the ground beyond the sideline's extension or the centre mark's extension, nor the ground more than 0.3 m from where each stood at the release (a foot fault); after the strike you may step anywhere on your side. A serve that touches the net and still lands in the box is a let: it does not count, and you serve again, under the same rules, with the spare ball, which you take from its cup with your left gripper (before a let you may not touch it); this happens at most 1 time(s), a further let fails. You fail on any fault: releasing the ball when not at rest in the server's position, a foot fault, the tossed ball touching you for more than 0.05 s (caught again: one toss per serve), touching anything but you and the racket (the spare ball, its cup or stand, the net), reaching the ground or passing the net unstruck, the racket striking the ball before it is clear of you, a ball that reaches the racket faster than 5.0 m/s, a hit that does not speed it up, the racket touching the struck ball again (contacts less than 0.05 s apart are one hit) or staying on it longer than 0.05 s, the served ball touching you (your grippers included), a net post, the ground on your side or the ground outside the service box, or passing the net's plane anywhere but over the net. You also fail if the racket goes unheld (dropped, thrown, or slipped until the jaws no longer touch the handle), if any part of you or the racket touches the net or a post, if you touch the spare ball before a let, if you fall, or after 20 s. A fall is: your pelvis lower than 0.35 m above the ground, or any part of you other than your feet touching the ground; the racket may touch the ground.
