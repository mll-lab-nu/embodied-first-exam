## Task
Return the serve: after the tennis ball has bounced once in your service box, hit it with the racket held in your right hand's gripper back over the net so that it lands in the singles court on the far side, without letting go of the racket. (Embodied Olympics `tennis-return-realgrasp`; the robot as a service on a clock that does not stop, slowdown S = 1: its head cameras, joint sensors and IMUs, and the venue's fixed camera only.)

## Difficulty
The ball crosses the whole court in about a second and is still moving at more than 15 m/s when it reaches you, so the racket has to be in a path a few centimetres wide at an instant you must predict, and a robot that only walks cannot go far to meet it. The racket is held only by a two-finger gripper's friction: a hard swing and the ball's impact twist it in the grip, so the face the ball meets is not where the arm put it, and a swing strong enough to return the ball over the net must not fling the racket out of the hand. The scene is dynamic (`scripts/task_dynamics.toml`: The ball is served toward the robot at serve_time_s with spin.), and in standard-realtime mode time does not stop: once an attempt's clock starts the venue's timed events happen whatever the agent does, so it has to plan or stream the robot's rows from a program of its own, with no simulator and no state, and 10 resets (only the last attempt is graded).

## Reference solution
`solution/oracle_plan.py` plans the privileged twin's reference rows (`solution/rows.npz`) into the attempt at the control steps where they ran there (`submit(..., at=step)`; the first, at step 0, starts the clock); it uses no privileged information at run time. reference solution: walks 0.7 m on the walking policy, backswing while walking, a one-handed backhand at zero command with the free 0.30 kg racket held only by the right Dex1-1 at 120 N (head 7.1 m/s, ball 14.7 in -> 15.1 out, 44 deg; the racket turns 51 deg in the grip and stays); frozen start + 9/10 perturbed starts (4 cm, 0.03 rad) succeed, replay bit-exact. Second positive: a real stand-mode forehand with only the robot's place injected (19.5 m/s out, 12 deg). Negatives: two bounces, racket dropped / thrown / out of the hand in the ball's path, ball on the gripper and on the forearm, volley, double hit, carry, own side, long, wide, net, through the net, line in/out, racket on the net post, body at the net, far side, racket on the waiting ball, a fall. Held-racket impacts measured: e_app 0.47 at 15 m/s, 0.39 at 30; an 8 cm off-axis hit turns the racket 47 deg (15 m/s) / 137 deg (30 m/s) about its handle

## Verification
The sim sidecar (`rcb_realtime.py` + `rcb_service.py`, the shared eai-standard/2.3 layer, with the standard twin's
`server.py`) runs the attempt on its clock and its referee judges it live; every control step the simulator applied is
recorded, and the live referee's call on the last attempt is the verdict (`final.json`: `graded_by` live, `replays` 0; no
grading replay, owner 2026-10-17, board 0151); the video is rendered from the recorded attempt afterwards.
`/tests/grade.py` reads `/data/final.json`: pass iff the live attempt succeeded. The clock's record
(`stalls`, `max_lag_s`, ...) is copied into `reward.json` and decides nothing. `nop`: the attempt never starts (its
clock starts at the first submit), reward 0. The
rules: The court is the ITF singles court: 23.77 m long and 8.23 m wide, measured to the outside of the lines, with a service line 6.4 m from the net on each side. The net stands across the middle, 0.914 m high at its centre and rising in a straight line to 1.07 m at the posts, which stand 0.914 m outside the sidelines. The ball is the ITF ball (radius 0.0335 m, 0.0577 kg, with air drag and spin lift). The racket is a separate body, 0.685 m long and 0.3 kg, balanced 0.33 m from the butt, with a strung surface 0.33 m by 0.25 m and an octagonal handle 0.0335 m across its flats and 0.19 m long; at the start its handle is closed in your right hand's two-finger gripper, which holds it by friction only: it can slip, turn in the gripper and fall. The racket is in your hand while its handle touches both of the gripper's jaws; it has left your hand when for more than 0.05 s the handle has not touched both jaws. 3.0 s after the start the ball is served from behind the far baseline at 30.0 m/s into your right service box. In order: the ball bounces once on your side; the racket, in your hand, hits it (the strings, the frame or any other part of the racket counts); its centre passes the net's plane from your side to the far side, above the net's top edge or outside a post (it may touch the net or a post on the way); and its first contact with the ground after that is inside the far half of the singles court, the lines included (the point under the ball's centre decides; ground contacts less than 0.02 s apart are one bounce): that is the success. You fail if the racket leaves your hand at any time (dropped, thrown or slipped out of the gripper), if the racket touches the ball before its bounce (a volley), if the ball bounces a second time on your side before your hit, if after your hit the ball touches the ground on your side or outside the far court, if the ball touches any part of you (the gripper included) at any time after the serve, if the racket touches the ball a second time (contacts less than 0.05 s apart are one hit) or stays on it longer than 0.05 s, if the ball passes the net's plane between the posts below the net's top edge, if any part of you or the racket touches the net or a post, or the ground beyond the net's plane, if you or the racket touch the ball before it is served, if you fall, or after 10 s. A fall is: your pelvis lower than 0.35 m above the ground, or any part of you other than your feet touching the ground; the racket may touch the ground on your side as long as it stays in your hand.
