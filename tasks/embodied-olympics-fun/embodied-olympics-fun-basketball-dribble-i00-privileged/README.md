## Task
Dribble the basketball, which starts held in your two hands, from the end line across the centre line, 14 m up the court, one hand at a time, without a dribbling, carrying or travelling violation. (Embodied Olympics `basketball-dribble`: one Unitree H1-2 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
The ball keeps 85 % of its speed in a bounce but a hand absorbs it, so every touch has to push it down and forward again by the hand's own motion to where the hand will be, with a hand about 8 cm wide and a ball 24 cm across, for 14 m, on a body whose walking gait covers about 0.7 m/s, drifts in heading, and is disturbed by every push of the arm. The built-in walking gait steps on the spot even at a zero command, so the ball has to leave the hands before it starts.

## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in one fresh process (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success. The rules: A FIBA court, 28.0 m by 15.0 m inside its boundary lines (lines 0.05 m wide); the centre line's axis is 14.0 m from the end line. The ball is a size-7 basketball (0.239 m across, 0.62 kg, it keeps 0.85 of its speed in a bounce on the floor). Your hands are five-finger hands, each closed by its grip value (0 open, 1 closed); they hold the ball only by contact and friction: it starts resting on your upturned right hand, the fingers curled a little round it, your left hand on its side, and it can slip or drop. A hand is the palm, the fingers, the thumb and the wrist; a hand is on the ball while it touches the ball, gaps shorter than 0.03 s included; the ball is in your hands while at least one hand is on it, and it has left them once no hand has been on it for 0.03 s. You stand still. Starting the dribble: the first foot you lift makes the other your pivot foot; the pivot foot, and before a foot is lifted either foot, may not slide more than 0.2 m along the floor, and the pivot foot may not leave the floor before the ball has left your hands (a foot has left the floor when it has not touched it for 0.03 s). While the ball is in your hands it may touch no part of you but your hands, and it may not touch the floor. The ball leaving your hands (pushed, dropped or slipped) is the first touch of your dribble. The dribble: after that you play the ball with one hand at a time, by touches that push it to the floor (thrown, tapped, rolled or bounced): between two touches by your hands the ball must have touched the floor; contacts of a hand less than 0.03 s apart are one touch. There is no limit to your steps while the ball is not in a hand. You succeed at the first moment the ball touches the floor in the frontcourt (beyond the centre line's far edge, 14.0 m plus half a line width from the end line) with both your feet in the frontcourt (every point where each foot last touched the floor is beyond that edge), after at least one touch of your dribble after the ball left your hands. You fail if you travel at the start; if the ball touches the floor or a part of you other than a hand while it is in your hands; if, after it has left your hands, a hand's touch lasts longer than 0.3 s (the ball has come to rest in the hand); if a hand carries the ball (it touches the ball from below, its lowest contact point on the ball lower than the ball's centre by more than 0.3 of the ball's radius, for longer than 0.1 s); if both hands touch the ball at the same time; if a hand touches the ball twice without the ball having touched the floor in between; if the ball touches any part of you other than a hand; if the ball's centre is ever more than 2.0 m (horizontally) from your pelvis after it left your hands; if the ball or any part of you touches the floor on or outside a boundary line (out of bounds); if you fall (a fall: your pelvis lower than 0.35 m above the floor, or any part of you other than your feet on the floor); or after 60 s. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §4).
