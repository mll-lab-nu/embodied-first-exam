## Task
Standard-mode twin of `robotangle-hedgehog-in-cage-easier-i00-privileged` (same frozen instance): the agent gets only the `eai-standard/2.4` client
(docs/STANDARD_MODE_2_2.md); the simulator runs in the `sim` sidecar (`environment/docker-compose.yaml`, GPU on the
sidecar), which owns the scene and records the episode as a (T, 8) trajectory; its collect hook
(`environment/sim/finalize.sh`) replays the trajectory once in a fresh process for success, rendering the video during
that replay. The verifier runs in a separate sandbox (`tests/Dockerfile`) over the sidecar's artifacts. Compare against
the privileged task to measure the cost of losing resets, privileged state and the tooling's primitives.

## Difficulty
Same scene, instance and success rule as `robotangle-hedgehog-in-cage-easier-i00-privileged` (see its Difficulty). In standard mode the agent loses resets, privileged state and planning primitives: it sees camera images and proprioception only, acts through the client, and gets one episode, so it has to estimate the scene from images and recover from its own mistakes without starting over.

## Reference solution
`solution/solve.sh` streams the recorded rows of the author's standard-mode oracle episode (`solution/oracle_qpos.npy`, one `joint_position` row per 25 ms control step, `hold`) through the client (`solution/oracle_replay.py`). That episode scored 1 (live and fresh replay) on merged-v18 (source 5b5677f0, tooling a9d09d66; lab run 20261006T112152-3799673). This family's task, geometry and asset files are unchanged from that build to this release; the shared simulator, tooling, graders and sim service changed only in paths that do not act on this family's scene (the cloth particle speed, a constraint solver chosen by name, the generated PBD birth state of dev/jianwen 2dafa5f2b), apart from the new report fields `initial_state_generated` and `initial_state`. The oracle was not re-run on this build (docs/benchmarks/robotangle.md, Evidence).

## Details

### Goal
Separate and place the whole hedgehog ball, including every spike inside the green dashed goal region. Keep all cage components on the puzzle/system side.

### Success requirements
- Put every part of the whole hedgehog ball, including every spike inside the fixed goal volume, leaving at least 1 mm inside its horizontal border. The centre alone is insufficient; no tip, spike, ring segment or cord segment may remain outside.
- Keep the target above the tabletop (contact tolerance 1 mm below the surface) and within the goal volume's height. It need not touch the table. The goal volume extends 233.629 mm above the tabletop.
- Keep all cage components, and every other remaining puzzle component, entirely on the system side of the fixed separator. Moving the whole puzzle into the goal is not completion.
- Keep each target rigid part's linear speed at most 1 mm/s and angular speed at most 0.02 rad/s. Holding the target with the gripper is allowed; release is not required.
- Keep all final conditions satisfied together for at least 2 s of simulation time. If any final condition stops holding, the confirmation interval starts again. A brief qualifying pose is not enough; let the simulation advance through the full interval.

### Task failure conditions
- A task piece falling more than 100 mm below the tabletop ends the episode in failure. Finger contact with a held movable piece may penetrate at most 2 mm; holding requires both fingers of the same hand to contact the same piece, or an active assisted grasp by that hand. All other task contacts, including piece-piece, table, arm and gripper-housing contacts, may penetrate at most 1 mm. Exceeding either limit ends the episode in failure.

### Grader details (in addition to the requirements above)
- Goal square in this instance: the inner edge of the 2 mm wide dashed green line is the containment limit (the region's border runs along the middle of the line); it encloses a 241.6 mm x 241.6 mm square. Every point counts: for a rigid part every vertex of its visual and collision meshes.
- The separator is an invisible, fixed vertical plane parallel to the goal square's near edge, 5 mm beyond the point of all remaining components that was closest to the goal square at the start; the square's near edge is at least 30 mm beyond it. The remaining components here are fixed.
- Contact penetration as this benchmark grades it (it replaces the "at most 1 mm / 2 mm" wording above): penetration is recorded, it never ends the episode by itself. A success with deep interpenetration (a task contact deeper than 3 mm, or deeper than 1 mm for at least 0.25 s: 100 consecutive physics ticks; for a finger contact with a held piece 4 mm, or 2 mm for 0.25 s) is not credited automatically but reviewed, with the recorded evidence and frames; a clean success is credited automatically. A task contact deeper than 10 mm is a simulator breakdown (below).
- Contact penetration is read from the contacts at the end of every physics tick (2.5 ms; 10 ticks per control step). "Held" is decided anew at every tick: a zero-force touch counts, and if one finger loses contact (with no active assisted grasp), the other finger is back at the non-held limits in the same tick. The cage is fixed, so finger contact with it is never held (always the non-held limits).
- Robot safety (this benchmark's tooling): no gripper target goes lower than 15 mm below the tabletop; a commanded target that would bring any part of a gripper lower is stopped there, on the commanded path. The fingertips reach about 6 mm beyond the tool frame (TCP) along its approach axis when open and about 19 mm when closed (the Robotiq fingertips move forward as they close). Arm drive torques are capped (the effort limits in robot_spec), so an arm pushing against an obstacle stalls at that torque.
- Forcing is recorded, not judged: an arm drive (not a gripper drive) at 95% of its torque cap or more while that arm's gripper, or a part it holds, presses on anything else with 20 N or more in total is forcing; 0.5 s of it on every physics tick is a forcing event in the evidence (`meta["safety"]`), shown to the reviewer when a success is reviewed. Forcing does not end the episode and does not change the grade by itself.
- Simulator breakdown (a non-finite state or a task contact deeper than 10 mm): the episode ends as an infrastructure error, not your failure, unless an arm was forcing (even for one tick) within the 0.25 s before it: then it is your failure (forced_breakdown: what broke and what forced it).
- Grasp regions (assisted grasping, see Grippers above; one sphere per graspable part): hedgehog_ball: radius 89.3 mm, enclosing the whole part. Both finger contacts must lie inside the part's sphere; a part touched elsewhere is grasped physically.

### Change Log
- 2026-10-08: base image 0.2.19 -> 0.2.21, now public. The simulator, the frozen instances and the audited libuipc wheel are unchanged: on compute capability 8.9 and 12.x (e.g. RTX 4090, L40S, RTX PRO 6000) the physics runs the same libuipc code as before. New: the NVIDIA Vulkan ICD and EGL vendor files, and a second libuipc build of the audited recipe for compute capability 8.6 (RTX A6000 class), which the tooling selects at Session start on every other GPU, so knot_untying and rope_threading run there too. The runtime binding names both builds, and the tooling hash changes with the selection. The compose files reserve one GPU without `runtime: nvidia`, which with the image's NVIDIA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=all could expose every GPU (Nyx needs exactly one). The standard grader counts a fresh replay that crashed without writing its verdict (e.g. CUDA out of memory) as a grader error; it counted as instance_mismatch. The scene, frozen instance and pass rule are unchanged, so the task keeps its name.
- 2026-10-08 (2): the instance now also starts on a CPU of another instruction-set class. The asset generators run on numpy and OpenBLAS, whose SIMD kernels depend on the CPU: on a host without AVX-512 (e.g. AWS L4 / L40S instances) the pinned generator writes the frozen asset files with other bytes (floating-point noise up to 1e-8 m, face order), and the Session refused the instance (IPC and stored PBD states also refused their archive). Such a host now realizes the frozen recipe (`instance_realized`): the same asset files from the generator the runtime binding pins, archives that differ from this scene only in those bytes, and the frozen state, exactly or within the pose tolerance; the live episode and the fresh replay realize it alike. The graders and the sim service record `instance_realized` and `assets_exact`. A host that reproduces the frozen bytes runs exactly as before. The tooling hash changes; the image, scene, frozen instance and pass rule are unchanged, so the task keeps its name.
