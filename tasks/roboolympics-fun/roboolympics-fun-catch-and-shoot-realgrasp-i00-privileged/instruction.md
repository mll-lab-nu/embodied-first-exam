# roboolympics catch-and-shoot-realgrasp — instance i00

You control a simulated Unitree H1-2 humanoid robot (27 joints, 1.78 m tall, 67 kg; the RoboOlympics simulator on MuJoCo)
via Python. **Task:** Catch and shoot: catch the chest pass that comes to you from the left wing between your two palms, then shoot from where you stand so that the ball enters the basket from above.

This is a *fixed instance*: the same venue and start every time. You may reset and retry as many times as you want.
Your deliverable is **one replayable trajectory**, not a general policy.

## The venue
A FIBA court. You stand at the top of the key facing the basket (+x), your arms at rest and your hands empty: the point under the ring's centre is 5.8 m ahead of your pelvis (inside the three-point line; the free-throw line is 1.575 m ahead of you). The passer stands on the left wing: the ball waits 1.05 m ahead of you and 4.96 m to your left, 1.25 m above the floor. The pass is aimed at the point 0.4 m straight ahead of where your pelvis starts, 1.25 m above the floor: it leaves on an upward arc (28 degrees) and is there 0.76 s later, moving at about 7.2 m/s to your right and downwards. Your hands have no grip: the grip columns of the action rows do nothing in this task.

Frame: metres; the origin is on the floor under your pelvis at the start, +x is the way you face at the start, +y is
to your left, +z is up; the floor is z = 0. 

## The rules
**Rule book:** FIBA Official Basketball Rules 2024: Art. 2 (court), Art. 3 and the Basketball Equipment appendix (backboard, ring, size-7 ball), Art. 13 (how the ball is played: with the hands), Art. 14 (control: a player holds a live ball), Art. 16 (goal; a ball that passes through the basket from below is a violation), Art. 25 (travelling). Where this task departs from it:

- Art. 3 (equipment): the hands are flat rubber palm pads without fingers and have no grip; the ball is caught and held only by pressing it between the two palms (friction), and it slips or drops when the arms do not press hard enough. Any shooting style is allowed (a two-handed set or push shot, an underhand two-handed shot); the ball is released when no hand touches it any more.
- Art. 3 (equipment, the hand): the palm is modelled as the soft part of a hand: it absorbs the ball (a ball thrown at a still palm keeps 0.12 of its speed) instead of bouncing it like the floor (0.85); with the ball's own floor rebound on the palms a squeezed ball rattles out of them within about a second. The floor, the ring and the backboard keep the ball's rule-book rebound.
- Art. 14 (control) has no duration: here the ball is caught when both your hands have been on it together for 0.1 s without a break (a two-palm hold that lasts, not a tap that passes it on).
- Art. 13.1.2 (the ball is played with the hands only) is read strictly: from the pass until the shot is through, the ball may touch no part of you but your hands (palm pads and wrists); trapping it against the trunk, the arms or a leg is not a catch of this task, and a touch by a leg or a foot (always taken as deliberate: intent cannot be judged) is a violation.
- Basketball Equipment (basket): the ring is rigid (no pressure release) and the net is only drawn: it does not slow the ball. A goal is judged at the ring: the ball's centre down through the ring's plane and on to one ball diameter below it.
- The pass is thrown by a machine, not a team-mate: always the same chest pass, at a fixed time.
- Art. 25.2.1: a ball caught with a foot off the floor is judged by the two-step rule without the pivoting it allows after a stop: after two steps no foot may touch the floor again while the ball is held.
- One attempt, no opponent, no rebound: a pass that is not caught on the fly, and touching the ball after the shot's release, end the attempt.

The official basket: a rigid ring of 0.45 m inner diameter (0.018 m metal), its top edge 3.05 m above the floor, its centre 0.375 m in front of a backboard 1.8 m wide and 1.05 m high whose lower edge is 2.9 m above the floor. The ball is a size-7 basketball (0.239 m across, 0.62 kg, it keeps 0.85 of its speed in a bounce on the floor); it flies in air (drag and spin lift). It is passed to you 2.0 s after the start: a chest pass of 8.0 m/s from 5.0 m away. Your hands have no fingers and no grip: you can hold the ball only by pressing it between your two flat palms, and only the force of your arms and friction hold it; it can slip out. A hand is the palm pad and the wrist; a hand is on the ball while it touches the ball, gaps shorter than 0.03 s included; the ball is in your hands while at least one hand is on it. In order: you catch the ball before it has touched the floor (the catch: both your hands have been on the ball together for 0.1 s without a break; touches before that, of one hand or both, are allowed); from the catch the ball stays in your hands until you let go of it (the shot's release: the moment no hand is on it; a ball that slips or drops out of your hands is released too); its centre goes down through the ring's plane inside the ring and on down to one ball diameter below it, which is the success. Travelling, from the catch to the shot's release: if both your feet are on the floor at the catch, the first foot you lift makes the other your pivot foot; the pivot foot, and before a foot is lifted either foot, may not slide more than 0.2 m along the floor (in the steps case below: any foot that is on the floor, from where it stood or was put down), and once it has left the floor no foot may touch the floor again before the ball has left your hands. If a foot is off the floor at the catch, you may take at most 2 steps (a step: a foot touching the floor after it has been off it for at least 0.03 s; both feet landing within 0.03 s of each other are one step; not the same foot twice in a row), and after them no foot may touch the floor again before the release. You fail if anything of you touches the ball before it is passed; if, from the pass until the release, the ball touches any part of you other than your hands; if the passed ball touches the floor before you have caught it; if you travel; if any part of you touches the ball more than 0.05 s after the shot's release (it cannot be taken again); if the ball touches the floor before it is through the ring; if the ball's centre comes up through the ring from below; if you fall before the ball is through (a fall: your pelvis lower than 0.35 m above the floor, or any part of you other than your feet on the floor); or after 15 s.

A referee decides this from the simulator's state after every physics step (1 ms): the events that must happen, in
order, are **catch → release → through the ring**. The attempt ends at the success (that moment is the success), at the first rule violation,
or after 15 s (1500 control steps). `s.status()` shows the referee's record at any time:
`stages`, `failed` (the rule that ended the attempt), `metrics`, `events`.

## Tooling (already installed; run `python` from any directory)
```python
from rcb_rfo import Session
s = Session()                          # /app/instance.json; under a second
s.action_types                         # every action type: dim, layout, lower, upper, meaning (table below)
typ, row = s.hold()                    # the row that keeps the command in force with zero velocity (stand still)
done = s.step("walk", row)             # one control step (10 ms) of one row; True once the attempt is over
s.rows                                 # the sim rows of every step since the last reset: what save_trajectory() writes
s.status()                             # the referee: success, done, failed, stages, progress, metrics, events
s.state()                              # privileged state: pelvis (x, y, z, yaw), centre of mass, joints, the palms,
                                       # what each hand holds, and the task's objects under "task"
s.world                                # everything: s.world.m, s.world.d (MuJoCo model and data), s.world.robot
                                       # (addresses, limits), s.world.ref (the referee), s.world.task (the rules)
s.image("head_left")                   # a camera's RGB image (270 x 480; the cameras: `head_left`, `head_right`, `venue`)
s.proprio()                            # the robot's own sensors as one row (s.ep.proprio_layout)
s.reset()                              # back to the frozen start (unlimited, instant); clears s.rows
s.replay()                             # what the verifier does with s.rows, here, in a fresh world
s.save_trajectory()                    # writes the SCORED file /app/output/trajectory.npz from s.rows
```
The simulator is deterministic on the CPU: the same rows from the frozen start give the same state, so a trajectory you
generate closed-loop (reading `s.state()`, `s.world`) replays exactly. `python /opt/tools/rcb_rfo.py replay --instance
/app/instance.json --traj /app/output/trajectory.npz --out /tmp/r.json` runs the verifier's replay from the shell. The
world's source is in `/opt/tools/rfo/` (the robot and action layer `world.py`, the referee's toolkit `referee.py`) and
this task's rules, as code, in `/opt/tools/rfo_task.py`; the robot library (model, meshes, limits, the walking and
running policies' weights) is in `/opt/roboolympics/`. You may build your own copies of the world to plan or learn in.

## Actions
One row is one control step (10 ms; the physics runs at 1 kHz). Every row ends with `grip_left`, `grip_right`.

| type | row (then `grip_left`, `grip_right`) | floats | who drives what |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stand` | waist 1 + arms 14 joint targets | 17 | the platform holds the legs in the stance; you give the waist's 1 and the arms' 14 joint targets (rad) |
| `walk` | `vx`, `vy`, `wz` + waist 1 + arms 14 joint targets | 20 | Unitree's walking policy drives the legs from the velocity command (vx forward, vy to the left, m/s; wz turning left, rad/s; the robot's own frame); you give the waist and arm joint targets |
| `joints` | all 27 joint targets | 29 | you give all 27 joint targets (rad); the default PD gains, no feed-forward |
| `lowcmd` | all 27 joints × (`q`, `dq`, `tau`, `kp`, `kd`): 27 targets, then 27 velocities, 27 torques, 27 kp, 27 kd | 137 | Unitree's LowCmd for all 27 joints: position targets (rad), velocity targets (rad/s), feed-forward torques (N m), kp, kd; the motor torque is kp (q - q_measured) + kd (dq - dq_measured) + tau, evaluated at 1 kHz |

Velocity commands are in the robot's own frame (`vx` forward, `vy` to its left, m/s; `wz` turning left, rad/s). Bounds: `walk` |vx| ≤ 0.8, |vy| ≤ 0.5, |wz| ≤ 1.57. Joint targets are absolute angles (rad). Values outside the bounds are clipped. `grip_left`, `grip_right`: ≥ 0.5 closes the hand's grip, < 0.5 opens it. At the start the robot stands in `stand` mode with the arms at rest: the `stand` row with the table's start angles and both grips open keeps it there.

What the platform does and does not do for you:

- **`stand`** holds the legs at the standing pose with stiff gains and a gravity-support torque. It is not a balance
  controller: it does not step and it has no feedback. After an arm movement the body sways a few centimetres for a
  few seconds; a hard or fast swing of the arms, a push or a load can tip the robot over within a second or two.
- **`walk`** runs Unitree's own H1-2 walking policy (50 Hz) on the legs. At the largest forward command (0.8) it walks at
  about 0.6 m/s and its heading drifts (close the loop on the IMU); `vy` up to 0.5 and `wz` up to 1.57 rad/s. At a
  zero command it marks time on the spot and recovers from small disturbances, which `stand` does not. When the legs
  come to it from another mode it first blends them to the stance (0.3 s) and holds a zero command until the robot is
  steady (up to 2 s). It was trained with the arms at their zero pose (upper arms down, forearms forward): moving the
  arms disturbs it. This robot has no running policy: anything faster than a walk is yours to build or to find. The
  G1's two other walking policies (FALCON, AGILE) are not offered for it: driven on this robot (joints matched by name)
  they keep it upright but do not walk it.
- **`joints` / `lowcmd`** are yours: each motor's torque is `kp (q − q_measured) + kd (dq − dq_measured) + tau`, evaluated
  at 1 kHz with your row held for its 10 ms, then limited by the motor's torque-speed curve and torque limit. `joints`
  uses the default gains of the table and zero `dq` and `tau`; `lowcmd` takes yours (kp up to 1500 / 800 / 400 and kd up to
  30 / 20 / 10 for legs / waist / arms). Nothing balances the robot in these modes but your own targets.
- **Every joint target** is clipped into the joint's range and moves at most the joint's speed limit × 10 ms per step
  from the target in force. Joints a type does not name keep their targets. The speed limit is a limit on targets;
  what bounds the joint itself is the motor's torque-speed curve (a `lowcmd` feed-forward torque can turn a joint up to
  about 1.15 times that speed, where the motor's torque has fallen to zero).
- **The hands** have no working fingers here; each carries a fist pad (a box of 4.4 × 8 × 10 cm beyond the wrist, the
  10 cm along the forearm) with a grip: while `grip_left` / `grip_right` ≥ 0.5 the hand holds the nearest grippable
  object whose surface is within 3.5 cm of the palm point (the middle of the pad, 4.5 cm beyond the wrist flange along
  the forearm) and that moves at most 6 m/s relative to it; below 0.5 it lets go at once. Since the palm point is inside
  the pad, an object can be gripped where it touches one of the pad's two broad faces, not at its narrow sides or its
  far end. What is grippable is the task's own equipment, named in the rules.
- **A tool on a hand** (a racket, a paddle, a club; where the task has one) is fixed to the wrist in place of the pad.
  It collides with the world, the ball and the rest of your own body.
- **The air** is real: balls and implements feel drag and, when they spin, lift.
- **If the physics diverges** (a numerical blow-up, typically from a violent joint command), the attempt ends as a
  failure: "the simulation became unstable".
