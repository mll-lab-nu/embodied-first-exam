## Task
Do a high jump: approach the bar, take off from one foot, go over the bar between the uprights without knocking it off its supports, and come down on the landing mat. (Embodied Olympics `high-jump`: one Unitree H1-2 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
The bar is at the height of the robot's hip joints: the whole 67 kg body has to be thrown over it from one leg. The robot has no running or jumping gait at all; its walking gait reaches 0.7 m/s.

## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in one fresh process (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success. The rules: World Athletics rules for the high jump. The bar (4.0 m long, 0.03 m thick, 2.0 kg) rests with its upper side 1.0 m above the ground on two flat supports (0.04 m wide, 0.06 m long) that face each other from two uprights 4.02 m apart; pushed more than half a support's width forwards or backwards, or lifted, it falls. The bar lies across y in the plane x = 0; the plane the rules mean is that of its nearer edge, x = -0.03/2. Behind it lies the landing mat: 6.0 m wide, 4.0 m deep, 0.7 m high, soft, beginning 0.1 m behind that plane. The approach is free (any direction, any distance on the 15.0 m of level runway). A flight is a time of at least 0.05 s in which nothing of you touches anything but the bar. Your jump is the flight in which your pelvis crosses the bar's plane. Its take-off must be from one foot: in the last 0.15 s before lift-off your other foot presses on the ground with less than 0.05 of your weight (or it is off the ground for the whole of the take-off foot's last ground contact), and nothing else of you touches the ground; and in it your pelvis must rise at least 0.08 m above its height at lift-off. The clearance is judged in that one flight, between the take-off and your first contact with anything but the bar: your pelvis and both feet (the ankle joints) must cross the plane between the uprights and higher than the bar's upper side (one that comes back over the plane has not passed it and must pass it again), and every part of you must get beyond the plane. A crossing made while you touch the ground or the mat, or after the flight has ended, never counts. You have cleared the bar when all of that has happened in the flight. You may touch the bar, for at most 0.3 s in all, and it may push you with a normal impulse of at most 2.0 N s in all (a real bar falls under any weight: it does not carry you or throw you up), and after the clearance you may land on any part of your body, on the mat or not. You succeed when you have cleared the bar and landed, and 2.0 s later the bar still lies on its supports: touching both, its centre within half a support's width of its resting place along x and within 0.005 m of its resting height, and at rest (its centre slower than 0.05 m/s, turning slower than 0.5 rad/s); a bar knocked up or off counts as off. A fall is not a failure by itself. You fail if the bar drops more than 0.05 m at any time, or is not lying on its supports at that judgement; if you are in contact with the bar for more than 0.3 s in all, or it pushes you with more than 2.0 N s; if you touch the ground or the mat beyond the plane before you have cleared the bar, with or without a jump; if your jump's flight ends (you touch anything but the bar) before you have cleared the bar; if the take-off is not from one foot; if the flight is not a jump; if your pelvis or a foot passes the plane outside the uprights or not higher than the bar; if your landing after the clearance touches the ground on the take-off side; or after 60 s (the time allowed for a trial). No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §4).
