# DexHand: Open a jam jar and set the lid aside — MCP tools and shell

Open the jam jar with the robot's right hand and set its lid aside on the worktop. Scene (world frame, metres, z up, +x forward from the robot): the worktop top is at z = 0.75 and spans |x| <= 0.45, |y| <= 0.58. A filled 297 ml glass jam jar (550 g, 67 mm body diameter, 110 mm tall, rim at z = 0.86) stands upright on the worktop centred at x = -0.22, y = -0.08. Nothing holds the jar: it stands on its own weight and glass-on-worktop friction, which resists only about 0.13 N m of twisting torque, so the hand must turn the lid without turning, sliding or tipping the jar. Its red 64.8 mm twist-off steel lid (32 g) is closed. Jar and lid are free rigid bodies. The closure is a lug thread made of contact geometry only: four inclined glass ramps on the jar neck hold four inward lugs of the lid, so the closed lid cannot be pulled off. Turning the lid counter-clockwise seen from above (positive rotation about +z relative to the jar) slides the lugs up the ramps; at 1.049 rad they pass the ramp ends, and while they sit in the gaps between the ramps (turn between 1.049 and about 1.26 rad) the lid lifts off freely; turned further without lifting, the lugs run into the next ramps' leads, and turning clockwise from the closed position tightens and jams. The lid is lightly closed: no vacuum, seal adhesion or tightening torque is modelled, and the jam is part of the jar's rigid mass, not a fluid. Complete three stages in this order. (1) Unscrew: while the lid is still seated on the thread (lid origin less than 6 mm above its closed height and within 4 mm of the jar axis, in the jar frame), its counter-clockwise turn relative to the jar, unwrapped after every physics substep, reaches at least 1.049 rad. (2) Lift clear: afterwards, the lowest point of the lid skirt rises more than 30 mm above the jar rim. (3) Set aside: afterwards, for 50 consecutive controls (1 s), the lid touches the worktop and touches neither the hand nor the jar, is upright (tilt at most 0.15 rad), has its centre at least 90 mm horizontally from the jar axis, moves at most 0.02 m/s and turns at most 0.3 rad/s, while the jar touches the worktop, is not touched by the hand, is upright within 0.10 rad and moves at most 0.02 m/s. The episode ends with success at that moment; a partial sequence never counts. The episode ends immediately with failure, checked after every 2 ms physics substep, when: the jar tilts more than 15 degrees (jar_tipped), slides more than 30 mm horizontally from its start (jar_slid) or is lifted more than 15 mm (jar_lifted); the lid touches the worktop before stage 2 (lid_knocked_off); the lid drops: while it is in the air (its lowest point more than 10 mm above the worktop top), at any substep it touches nothing (no hand, jar or worktop contact) while its centre of mass moves downward faster than 0.3 m/s (about a 5 mm free fall; there is no window to catch it again), or its centre of mass moves downward faster than 1.0 m/s whatever it touches (lid_dropped); a lid that tips over or falls flat on the worktop (lowest point within 10 mm of the worktop top) has not dropped; the jar or the lid touches the floor, its origin falls more than 5 mm below the worktop top or leaves the worktop footprint (lid_off_table, jar_off_table) or the workspace box x in [-0.45, 0.45] m, y in [-0.40, 0.40] m, z in [0.73, 1.25] m (object_left_workspace); the lid leaves the thread upward while its lugs are not in the gaps between the ramps, i.e. its turn modulo pi/2 is outside [1.019, 1.291] rad (lid_forced_through_thread); the parked left arm or the left hand touches the jar or the lid (left_arm_contact; moving the left arm is not blocked, but the task is done by the right arm and hand); or the physics becomes unstable (unstable_physics). Later actions cannot undo a failure. Only robot actuator targets are allowed: the joints of the Marvin right arm and its hand (the left arm stays parked with its hand open), no object teleports, grasp welds, scripted object motion or direct object forces. Any robot part touching the jar or the lid counts as the hand touching it. The episode allows at most 3000 controls at 50 Hz; reaching that limit without success is a failure.

You control the right arm and right hand of a fixed Tianji Marvin robot: a stand with two 7-joint arms and a dexterous
hand on each wrist (the robot stands behind the worktop, its base centre at x = -0.56, y = 0.08 on the floor, facing
+x; shoulders at z = 1.121 m (1.221 m above the floor)). The left arm stays parked beside the torso with its hand open; the task is done
by the right arm and hand alone. Every arm and finger joint is a position servo inside the physics engine under native
force bounds; arm servos have stiffness 700 N m/rad, damping 48 N m s/rad and **no gravity compensation** (a held
target sags under the arm's weight unless your targets account for it). Turning, lifting, carrying and releasing the
lid require physical contact, and the jar is held by nothing but its own weight and friction on the worktop. The
controller runs at 50 Hz, with ten 2 ms physics substeps. At reset the right hand hangs open, palm down, about 0.1 m
above the lid with its fingers along +x; the left arm is at its parked pose.

## Robot tools (MCP server `efe-robot`) and their Python client `efe_robot`
Besides the robot service's own client (below), you get the robot through the tools of the MCP server `efe-robot` (in
your tool list as `efe-robot` tools, for example `mcp__efe-robot__move_robot`) and through the same tools as Python
functions: `import efe_robot` (`/opt/tools/efe_robot.py`, on `PYTHONPATH`; each function has the tool's name, arguments
and result, and its docstring is the tool's description), or `python3 /opt/tools/efe_robot.py <tool> '<JSON arguments>'`
from the shell. They all drive the one robot: use one route at a time. The calls of the service's client and the tools
that do the same:

| Client | Tool |
|---|---|
| `Robot()`, `r.spec()` | `describe_robot()`: the robot's parts, joints and limits, cameras, control period, action types and conventions, and the time left; the full spec is saved as a JSON file (`spec_file`) you can read |
| `r.get_obs(cameras, seconds, depth)` | `observe(cameras, seconds, depth)` |
| `r.submit(type, A, dt, interpolate)` | `execute_trajectory(space="native", action_type=type, native_rows=A, dt=dt, interpolate=...)`, or `move_robot` / `execute_trajectory` in the conventions below |
| `r.status()`, `meta[...]` | every tool result: `time_left_s`, `sim_time_s`, `done`, `reason` and the service's messages |
| `r.reset()` / `POST /reset` | `reset_episode()` |

**Conventions** of `move_robot`, `locomote` and `execute_trajectory` (spaces other than `native`): the robot base frame
at the start of each call (+X forward, +Y left, +Z up), metres and radians, quaternions `wxyz`, gripper 0 = open to 1 =
closed. Base frame of this robot: the Marvin stand origin (the floor point under its torso): +X the way the robot faces,
+Y to its left, +Z up. Space `native` sends rows in the robot's own action types exactly as the sections below describe
them (their frame, units and gripper values).

**This robot** (Tianji Marvin stand with two 7-joint arms and a Sharpa Wave dexterous hand (22 finger joints) on each
wrist): right_hand (hand: 22 joints, no end-effector pose); left (arm: 7 joints, no end-effector pose); right (arm: 7
joints, no end-effector pose); left_hand (hand: 22 joints, no end-effector pose). Cameras: `overview`, `closeup`,
`side`, `wide`.

- `describe_robot()`: call it first.
- `observe(cameras?, depth?, seconds?, save_to?, inline_cameras?)`: images are written as JPEG files under `/app/obs/`
  (depth as `.npy`, float32 metres; with `seconds` > 0 a video `.mp4` too); the result has their paths, each camera's
  intrinsics `K` and pose in the robot base frame, each part's joints, gripper and end-effector pose, and `proprio_now`
  (the robot's own proprioception row; its layout is in the spec file). Open an image with your file-reading tool;
  `inline_cameras` also returns small images in the result.
- `locate(camera, u, v | pixels, window_px?)`: the 3-D point in the robot base frame that a camera sees at pixel (u, v)
  of its full-size image (u the column, v the row), or one per pixel of `pixels` [[u, v], ...]: from a new depth image
  of that camera and its calibration (no time passes). For a camera with depth and a known pose; for any other the error
  says so.
- `move_robot(goals, duration_s?)`: one goal per part,
  `{target, position?, quaternion_wxyz?, joint_positions?, gripper?}`; the parts move together and the call returns when
  the motion is done, with `reached` (errors per goal), a `failure` category if a goal was not reached, the state and
  the path of a frame per camera.
- `execute_trajectory(space, rows | native_rows | rows_file, dt?, interpolate?)`: a dense trajectory, rows `dt` seconds
  apart. `rows_file` is the path of a file under `/app` that holds the rows: a `.npy` (2-D float array) or `.csv` (one
  row per line, comma-separated) of native rows for space `native` (with `action_type`), or a `.json` list: of number
  arrays (native rows) or of row objects as in `rows` (the other spaces). Long arrays go this way rather than inline.
- `wait(seconds?, operation?)`: let time pass holding the current command, or collect a pending operation.
- `reset_episode()`: a new episode from the same starting state (it uses one reset of the budget).
- `signal(kind, note)`: `done` records that you are finished (the episode itself ends as described above).
- One tool call runs at a time (the Python functions wait for a pending call themselves). A call still executing after
  45 s returns `{"pending": true, "operation": id}` while the robot goes on executing it: call `wait(operation=id)` (it
  waits up to 45 s more each time) for its result. A result with `"episode_over": true` means the episode has ended.

**Notes on this robot.** Two 7-joint arms (parts left, right) and two hands (left_hand, right_hand), all joint
positions: no end-effector pose command and no inverse kinematics; the measured hand-mount pose comes back as ee. The
arm servos have no gravity compensation (a held target sags about 1 cm at the hand under load). A one-hand task keeps
the other arm parked at its start targets. Every column is a position target (servo); grasping and carrying work only
through contact. The parts follow the Sharpa Wave layout (describe_robot spec_file: hand "sharpa"). With the Wuji hand
(hand "wuji", 20 finger joints per hand) the columns differ: use execute_trajectory space native with the order in spec
action_types.joint_position.layout. Rows are 0.02 s apart or a multiple of it; a call covers at most 10 s. Measured
poses are in the world frame of the task text, quaternions wxyz.

## Hands
Two genuine manufacturer hand models are available: `sharpa` (Sharpa Wave, 22 finger actuators; the default) and `wuji` (Wuji Hand 2.2, 20 finger actuators). The task, scene, mount, objects and success predicate are the same for both; only one hand is used per episode.
Each row starts with the 14 Marvin arm joint targets (left arm J1-J7, then right arm J1-J7), followed by the left hand's finger targets and then the right hand's (22 per hand for `sharpa`, 20 per hand for `wuji`); the left arm is parked and only the right arm/hand perform the task.
The robot service runs the hand named by `DEXHAND_HAND` on the sim service (default `sharpa`); the agent cannot switch it during an episode. `spec()["hand"]` names it, and `spec()["action_types"]["joint_position"]` gives the row width (`58` for `sharpa`, `54` for `wuji`) and the exact order. The episode record names the hand; the service grades the episode it ran with that hand.

## Episodes and resets
You have **20 resets** (`spec["budget"]["reset"]`, which is authoritative: if it says 0, there is no reset
and the one episode is the run). A reset puts the robot and every object back in the same frozen starting state and
starts a new episode, either abandoning the running one or after one has ended; it takes no simulated time and
returns the start's images and proprioception:
```python
res = r._post("/reset", {"cameras": ["overview"]}, [], 900)   # POST /reset (the 2.2 client has no reset())
res.meta["episode"], res.meta["resets_left"]
```
Once an episode has ended, `submit` raises `EpisodeOver` until you reset. **Only the last episode is graded**: an
episode you reset away is never graded, a success ends the run at once (no reset after it), and when your time is up
the episode then running is the one graded. The wall clock is the run's and never restarts; `meta["episode"]` and
`meta["resets_left"]` count the episodes (`meta["control_steps"]` is the current episode's,
`meta["control_steps_total"]` all of them).

## Robot service
The simulator runs in a separate service. You receive camera RGB, optional calibrated depth, and the robot's own
joint positions, velocities, actuator efforts, wrist pose and current targets. The service serves no jar or lid pose,
contact, lid turn or stage; it reports only whether the episode has ended and whether in success or failure
(`task_failure`, without the failure's name). An episode ends at the first success or the first failure (see the task
text); after that the service accepts no more actions until a reset (see `## Episodes and resets`). The service records every action and watch step, then judges the graded episode as it ran
(that is the verdict; nothing is replayed). Leave your controller at `/app/output/gen.py`; no trajectory hand-in is
needed.

```python
from rcb_client import Robot
r = Robot()
spec = r.spec()
spec["hand"]                   # "sharpa" or "wuji"
obs = r.get_obs(cameras=["overview"], depth=True)
obs.last["overview"]           # RGB uint8 (480, 640, 3)
obs.last_depth["overview"]     # z-depth in metres
obs.meta["proprio"]            # named robot measurements
q = obs.group("joint_pos", now=True)
res = r.submit("joint_position", rows, dt=0.02, interpolate="hold", video="none")
r.fk(q)                       # robot-link forward kinematics; no physics is stepped
r.status()
res.save("/app/runs/001")
```
`rows` is `(T, D)` in `spec["action_types"]["joint_position"]["layout"]` order, all position targets in radians: the
14 Marvin arm joints (left arm `SHOULDER_PITCH_L_J1` ... `WRIST_ROLL_L_J7`, keep them at the parked start values;
then the right arm `..._R_J1` ... `..._R_J7`), then the left hand's and the right hand's finger joints. `D` is 58 for
`sharpa` (22 Sharpa Wave finger joints per hand, `left_thumb_CMC_FE` ... `left_pinky_DIP`, then `right_thumb_CMC_FE`
... `right_pinky_DIP`) and 54 for `wuji` (20 Wuji Hand 2.2 finger joints per hand, `l_wuji_l_THJ0..3` ...
`l_wuji_l_LFJ0..3`, then `r_wuji_r_THJ0..3` ... `r_wuji_r_LFJ0..3`). The arm servos have no gravity compensation. Actuator targets are clipped to the
published joint limits and clipping is reported. `dt` must be a multiple of 0.02 s, between 0.02 and 0.5 s; a chunk
covers at most 10 s. Hold interpolation holds each row for `dt`; linear interpolation starts from the previous target.
The robot has no scalar gripper opening. Quaternions in measured poses use WXYZ; world z is up, +x points forward from
the robot, the jar stands at (-0.22, -0.08) and the worktop top is at z = 0.75 m. Cameras are overview, closeup, side
and wide, at 640 x 480. Requests can return
RGB frames or mp4, with depth `none`, `last` or `frames`. Each call returns at most 1,000 camera images, counting
per-frame depth; reduce cameras, fps or duration for larger chunks. `get_obs(seconds=...)` holds the current targets
for up to 30 s and records those steps. Calling it without seconds only observes the current state. Forward kinematics
uses only the robot's own links; no IK primitive is supplied. If the service connection fails, call `r.wait_ready()`
and inspect `r.status()` before continuing. Crash recovery resumes the same journaled episode, without a reset, and the
wall-clock budget keeps running.

One call returns at most 1000 images (`max_frames_per_call`, PROTOCOL §3 "Images per call").

## Resources
Your container: **2 CPUs**, **4 GiB of memory**, **1024 processes and threads** and **10 GiB** of disk (new files under `/app`, `/tmp` and `/root`). Your container has no GPU. The simulator runs in its own container and does not count against these. CPU only slows you down; a process that goes over any other cap can be killed. Usage and caps: `/run/rcb-limits/usage.json`.

## Time limit
You have 3600 seconds to complete this task. Do not cheat by using online solutions or hints specific to this task.

This is wall-clock time from the start of your session; the time you wait for the simulator service counts. When it is up you are stopped without warning and the episode is graded as it stands at that moment. The service's `time_left_s` counts the same limit from your first request to the service, so it never runs out before your time does: your real time left is `time_left_s` minus the time you spent before that request. Plan by it. Your session started when you received this task. `/run/rcb-limits/usage.json`, rewritten every few seconds, gives the time left on this limit (`time_left_s`) and its end (`deadline_utc`), counted from a little before your session started, so it never gives you more time than you have: read it (or run `date`) before you decide that time is short, never guess. Waiting counts too: a tool call that sends input to a process that keeps running (an interactive interpreter, a simulator loop, a server) can wait for the whole wait or yield time you give it even when the answer is printed at once, so give such calls a short wait and poll again if the output is not complete, or run one-shot scripts that exit when they are done. Installing packages is not cheating (`## External material` above).
