# DexHand · Install a desktop DDR4 RAM module (standard)

## Task
One dexterous hand on a six-axis wrist stage (no arm), at a bench (DexHand, MuJoCo). Install the DDR4 memory module into
the outer DIMM socket of the motherboard (socket_3, the slot nearest the board edge): the module stands upright in a
slotted anti-static foam holder on the bench, key notch towards +y; the motherboard is fixed in its service tray and the
socket's single moving latch starts open. Pick the module up, keep its keyed orientation, insert it straight into the
socket and press it home so that the passive latch snaps closed, then let go.
The robot runs as a service you reach only through its client, as if it were real hardware.

## Goal
Success: in this order: the module is lifted out of the holder, held by the thumb and at least one other finger; it sits
fully down in the target socket, straight and the right way round (a reversed module never counts); the latch has
snapped closed; then the hand is off the module and the latch, and the module stays seated, latched and still for a
second. A partial installation never counts. What counts is how things stand at the end of the episode: a success
reached earlier and lost by the end does not count, so finish in the state you want graded and stop sending commands.

The episode ends at once as a failure if the module touches anything other than the hand, its foam holder or the target
socket (the bench, the service tray, the motherboard and its components, the other three sockets, the floor); is dropped
or falls freely (no drop-and-catch); drops below the bench top or is pushed into the socket's floor; leaves the work
area; or if the physics becomes unstable. Later actions cannot undo a failure.

Nothing to hand in: the service records and grades the episode. Keep the script that drives the robot at `/app/output/gen.py`.

Real-robot rules:
- Episodes: 20 resets (`spec()["budget"]["reset"]`), each a new episode from the same starting state; within an episode
  what you knock over stays knocked over until you reset. Only the last episode is graded: when your time is up the
  episode then running is graded.
- No privileged state: you get the robot's camera images (with their calibration) and its own sensor readings; no object
  poses, no scene layout, no success signal.
- Write your own controller; do not use the benchmark's demonstrations, trained policies or any published solution.

Only the robot's actuator targets act on the scene: no object teleports, grasp welds, scripted object motion or applied
forces.

### This run: no success signal
This run changes how the episode ends.

- The robot never tells you whether you have succeeded: reaching the goal changes no response, status, error or counter,
  and the episode does not end there.
- You decide when you are done: send `POST /finish` with an empty JSON body (`{}`) to the service, on the same base URL
  as your other calls (`http://sim:8000` or `http://localhost:8000`; in Python, for example
  `requests.post(base + "/finish", json={})`). The state at that moment is graded with the task's success rule (with the
  task's settle or hold time described above, run from that state). After `/finish` every call is refused (409).
- If you do not call it, the state when your session ends (you stop, or your time runs out) is graded.
- A success reached earlier and then undone does not count: only the state at the end is graded. An event the task
  records once it happens (for example a scored goal or a completed stage sequence) stays part of that state, also when
  one of the task's failure rules ends the episode after it. Resets work as described above, within the budget, and only
  the episode that ends with `/finish` or at the end of your session is graded.
- The episode still ends on its own on a simulator error, at the task's technical step limit (graded as it stands), and
  where the task's own rules end it as a failure (for example a fall), as described above.

## Robot Interface
### Client
`/opt/tools/rcb_client.py`:
```python
from rcb_client import Robot
r = Robot()
spec = r.spec()
spec["hand"]                   # "sharpa" or "wuji"
obs = r.get_obs(cameras=["overview"], depth=True)
obs.last["overview"]           # RGB uint8 (480, 640, 3)
obs.last_depth["overview"]     # z-depth in metres
obs.meta["proprio"]            # named robot measurements
q = obs.group("joint_pos", now=True)
res = r.submit("joint_position", rows, dt=0.02, interpolate="hold", video="none")
r.fk(q)                       # robot-link forward kinematics; no physics is stepped
r.status()
res = r._post("/reset", {"cameras": ["overview"]}, [], 900)   # POST /reset (this client has no reset())
```
Requests can return RGB frames or mp4, with depth `none`, `last` or `frames`. Each call returns at most 1,000 camera
images, counting per-frame depth; reduce cameras, fps or duration for larger chunks. `get_obs(seconds=...)` holds the
current targets for up to 30 s and records those steps. Calling it without seconds only observes the current state.
Forward kinematics uses only the robot's own links; no IK primitive is supplied. If the service connection fails, call
`r.wait_ready()` and inspect `r.status()` before continuing. Crash recovery resumes the same journaled episode, without
a reset, and the wall-clock budget keeps running.

Resets: `r._post("/reset", ...)` (`POST /reset`) starts a new episode from the same starting state, abandoning the
running one or after one has ended; it takes no simulated time and returns the start's images and proprioception. Once
an episode has ended, `submit` raises `EpisodeOver` until you reset. `meta["episode"]` and `meta["resets_left"]` count
the episodes; `meta["control_steps"]` is the current episode's, `meta["control_steps_total"]` all of them.

### Actions
`rows` is `(T, D)` in `spec["action_types"]["joint_position"]["layout"]` order: the wrist targets
`wrist_x, wrist_y, wrist_z` (metres, world position of the hand mount) and `wrist_rx, wrist_ry, wrist_rz` (radians,
serial XYZ joints), then the finger targets in radians. `D` is 28 for `sharpa` (22 Sharpa Wave finger joints
`right_thumb_CMC_FE` ... `right_pinky_DIP`) and 26 for `wuji` (20 Wuji Hand 2.2 finger joints `r_wuji_r_THJ0..3`,
`r_wuji_r_FFJ0..3`, `r_wuji_r_MFJ0..3`, `r_wuji_r_RFJ0..3`, `r_wuji_r_LFJ0..3`). Actuator targets are clipped to the
published joint limits and clipping is reported. `dt` must be a multiple of 0.02 s, between 0.02 and 0.5 s; a chunk
covers at most 10 s. Hold interpolation holds each row for `dt`; linear interpolation starts from the previous target.
The robot has no scalar gripper opening.

### Observations
You receive camera RGB, optional calibrated depth, and the robot's own joint positions, velocities, actuator efforts,
wrist pose and current targets. Quaternions in measured poses use WXYZ; world z is up, the bench top is at z = 0.825 m
and the target socket `socket_3` is at (0.576, 0.004, 0.8438) with its slot along world +y. Cameras are overview,
holder, socket, socket_side and wide, at 640 x 480.

### Hands
Two hand models: `sharpa` (Sharpa Wave, 22 finger actuators; the default) and `wuji` (Wuji Hand 2.2, 20 finger
actuators); the task is the same for both, and one hand is used per episode. The service runs the hand that
`DEXHAND_HAND` names on the sim service (default `sharpa`); you cannot switch it during an episode. The hand in use is
`spec()["hand"]`; `spec()["action_types"]["joint_position"]` gives the row width (`28` for `sharpa`, `26` for `wuji`)
and the exact order. Both hands hang from the same six-axis wrist stage, with the same wrist joints, ranges, gains and
start targets; the wrist targets are the world position (m) and serial XYZ angles (rad) of the hand mount.

### Robot
You control one dexterous hand mounted on a six-DoF wrist positioning stage (three prismatic and three serial revolute
joints, no arm). The stage and the fingers are position actuated in the physics engine under native force bounds;
grasping, carrying, inserting and releasing require physical contact. The controller runs at 50 Hz, with ten 2 ms
physics substeps.

The wrist targets `wrist_x, wrist_y, wrist_z` are world positions of the hand mount (metres) and
`wrist_rx, wrist_ry, wrist_rz` serial XYZ joint angles (radians, not a quaternion).

## Others
- The socket's throat leaves little play: 1.66 mm for the 1.27 mm module.

## Limits
- Time: 3600 s of wall-clock time from the start of your session; everything counts (waiting for the service too). At
  the end you are stopped without warning and the episode is graded as it stands. Time left:
  `/run/rcb-limits/usage.json` (`time_left_s`, `deadline_utc`); the service's `time_left_s` (in every reply) starts at
  your first request, so the file is the one to trust. Calls into a long-running process can block for their whole wait
  time: use short waits or one-shot scripts.
- Tokens: no separate limit.
- Network: your model's API and the package registries (PyPI, Debian / Ubuntu archives) only. You may install packages
  and read anything in your container, the simulator's and the benchmark's source included. Do not search for or
  download a ready-made solution (published or reference solutions, recorded demonstrations, trained policies); trials
  are reviewed.
- Resources: 2 CPUs, 10 GiB memory, 1024 processes and threads, 10 GiB disk (new files under `/app`, `/tmp`, `/root`),
  no GPU; the simulator runs in its own container and does not count. Over a cap a process can be killed (the CPU cap
  only slows you down); usage: `/run/rcb-limits/usage.json`.
- Resets: at most 20 (`spec()["budget"]["reset"]`), each a new episode from the same starting state.
- Steps: at most 3,000 control steps (60 s of simulated time) per episode; there the episode ends and is graded as it
  stands.
