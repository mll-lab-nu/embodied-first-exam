# DexHand · Put a folded letter into an envelope (standard)

## Task
Put the folded letter into the envelope.

## Goal
Success: the letter goes in through the mouth of the envelope until all but a sliver of it is inside, between the side
seams and the walls; then the hand has let go of the letter and the envelope, and both lie still for a second.

Any of these ends the episode at once as a failure, which later actions cannot undo:
- The letter or the envelope falls below the desk top, touches the floor, is dropped or leaves the desk.
- The letter is pressed through a wall, a seam or the mouth lip of the envelope.
- The envelope is pushed away or tilted.
- The robot's arm, stand or parked left hand touches the letter or the envelope.
- The physics becomes unstable.

When you are done, send `/finish` to end the episode and get it graded. Only the state at that point counts.

Rules:
- **No cheating.** Do not search for, download or use any existing solution (published or reference solutions, the
  benchmark's demonstrations, trained policies); trials are reviewed.
- Episodes: 20 resets (`spec()["budget"]["reset"]`), each a new episode from the same starting state; within an episode
  what you knock over stays knocked over until you reset. Only the last episode is graded. No reset is possible after
  `/finish`.
- You get only the cameras and the robot's own sensor readings: no object poses, no scene layout, no score, no success
  signal.

Only the robot's actuator targets act on the scene: no object teleports, grasp welds, scripted object motion or applied
forces.

## Robot Interface
The right arm and hand of a fixed Tianji Marvin robot (two 7-joint arms, a dexterous hand on each wrist; the left arm
stays parked), at a desk (DexHand, MuJoCo). The robot runs as a service you reach through its client or its MCP tools,
as if it were real hardware.

`POST /finish` (MCP tool `finish`): ends the episode; it is graded right away.

### Client
`/opt/tools/rcb_client.py`:
```python
from rcb_client import Robot
r = Robot()
spec = r.spec()
spec["hand"]                   # "sharpa" or "wuji"
obs = r.get_obs(cameras=["overview"], depth=True)
obs.last["overview"]           # RGB uint8 (480, 640, 3)
obs.last_depth["overview"]     # z-depth in metres
obs.meta["proprio"]            # named robot measurements
q = obs.group("joint_pos", now=True)
res = r.submit("joint_position", rows, dt=0.02, interpolate="hold", video="none")
r.fk(q)                       # robot-link forward kinematics; no physics is stepped
r.status()
res = r._post("/reset", {"cameras": ["overview"]}, [], 900)   # POST /reset (this client has no reset())
```
Requests can return RGB frames or mp4, with depth `none`, `last` or `frames`. Each call returns at most 1,000 camera
images, counting per-frame depth; reduce cameras, fps or duration for larger chunks. `hold(s)` holds the current targets
and records those steps. `get_obs()` only observes the current state. Forward kinematics uses only the robot's own links
(the robot base is at (1.15, -0.25, 0.10) m, facing -x); no IK primitive is supplied. If the service connection fails,
call `r.wait_ready()` and inspect `r.status()` before continuing. Crash recovery resumes the same journaled episode,
without a reset, and the wall-clock budget keeps running.

Resets: `r._post("/reset", ...)` (`POST /reset`) starts a new episode from the same starting state. You can call it
during a running episode, which it abandons, or after one has ended. It takes no simulated time and returns the start's
images and proprioception. Once an episode has ended, `submit` raises `EpisodeOver` until you reset. `meta["episode"]`
and `meta["resets_left"]` count the episodes. `meta["control_steps"]` counts the current episode's control steps,
`meta["control_steps_total"]` those of all episodes.

### MCP tools
- The same service is also an MCP server, `efe-robot` (in your tool list), with one tool per endpoint: `spec`,
  `observe`, `submit`, `reset`, `kinematics`, `finish`. A tool takes its endpoint's request fields (any other field in
  `fields`) and returns the service's reply `meta` unchanged; images, arrays and video are written as files under
  `/app/obs/` (`.png`, `.npy`, `.mp4`) and the result lists them. Long action chunks can be passed as a file
  (`actions_file`).
- `observe` returns at once with what is already recorded: the current frame, or the last `last_s` seconds. Time passes
  only through `submit`, which answers when its rows have run; a `submit` with `interpolate` "hold", `seconds` and no
  rows lets time pass with the robot holding still.
- A call still running after 45 s returns `pending` with an `operation` id; `observe(operation=id)` collects its reply.
- The client and the tools drive the same robot in the same episode; use either, or both.

### Actions
`rows` is `(T, D)` in `spec["action_types"]["joint_position"]["layout"]` order (radians): the 7 left-arm joint targets
`SHOULDER_PITCH_L_J1_target ... WRIST_ROLL_L_J7_target`, the 7 right-arm joint targets
`SHOULDER_PITCH_R_J1_target ... WRIST_ROLL_R_J7_target`, then the left-hand and the right-hand finger targets. `D` is 58
for `sharpa` (22 Sharpa Wave finger joints per hand, `left_thumb_CMC_FE` ... `right_pinky_DIP`) and 54 for `wuji` (20
Wuji Hand 2.2 finger joints per hand, `l_wuji_l_THJ0` ... `r_wuji_r_LFJ3`). Actuator targets are clipped to the
published joint limits and clipping is reported. `dt` must be a multiple of 0.02 s, between 0.02 and 0.5 s; a chunk
covers at most 10 s. Hold interpolation holds each row for `dt`; linear interpolation starts from the previous target.
The robot has no scalar gripper opening.

Each row starts with the 14 Marvin arm joint targets (left arm J1-J7, then right arm J1-J7), followed by the left hand's
finger targets and then the right hand's (22 per hand for `sharpa`, 20 per hand for `wuji`). The left arm is parked;
only the right arm and hand perform the task.

### Observations
You receive camera RGB, optional calibrated depth, and the robot's own joint positions, velocities, actuator efforts,
the hand root pose (`right_hand_C_MC`) and current targets. Quaternions in measured poses use WXYZ; world z is up and
the desk top is at z = 0.70 m. Cameras are overview, closeup and mouth (a low view of the envelope mouth), at 640 x 480.

### Hands
Two hand models: `sharpa` (Sharpa Wave, 22 finger actuators; the default) and `wuji` (Wuji Hand 2.2, 20 finger
actuators); the task is the same for both, and one hand is used per episode. The service runs the hand that
`DEXHAND_HAND` names on the sim service (default `sharpa`); you cannot switch it during an episode. The hand in use is
`spec()["hand"]`; `spec()["action_types"]["joint_position"]` gives the row width (`58` for `sharpa`, `54` for `wuji`)
and the exact order.

### Robot
You control the right arm and hand of a fixed Tianji Marvin dual-arm robot (a stand with two seven-joint arms and a
dexterous hand on each wrist). The robot stands at the desk's +x edge (base origin (1.15, -0.25, 0.10) m, facing -x; its
base plate stands on the floor at z = 0); the left arm is parked beside the torso and only the right arm and hand do the
task. Arm joints and fingers are position servos in the physics engine under native force bounds; pressing, sliding,
pushing and releasing require physical contact. The controller runs at 50 Hz, with ten 2 ms physics substeps.

Keep the left arm at its start targets; only the right arm and hand do the task.

## Limits
- Time: 3600 s of wall-clock time from the start of your session; everything counts (waiting for the service too). At
  the end you are stopped without warning. Time left: `/run/rcb-limits/usage.json` (`time_left_s`, `deadline_utc`); the
  service's `time_left_s` (in every reply) starts at your first request, so the file is the one to trust. Calls into a
  long-running process can block for their whole wait time: use short waits or one-shot scripts.
- Tokens: no separate limit.
- Network: your model's API and the package registries (PyPI, Debian / Ubuntu archives) only. You may install packages
  and read anything in your container, the simulator's and the benchmark's source included.
- Resources: 2 CPUs, 4 GiB memory, 1024 processes and threads, 10 GiB disk (new files under `/app`, `/tmp`, `/root`), no
  GPU; the simulator runs in its own container and does not count. Over a cap a process can be killed (the CPU cap only
  slows you down); usage: `/run/rcb-limits/usage.json`.
- Resets: at most 20 (`spec()["budget"]["reset"]`), each a new episode from the same starting state.
- Steps: at most 6,000 control steps (120 s of simulated time) per episode, then the episode ends.
- Finish: if you never send `/finish`, the episode is graded when the step limit is reached or your session ends.
