## Task
Run the 50 m hurdles over four free-standing hurdles: go over each one in your lane, never beside it, under its top, or after pushing it away, and bring your torso to the finish line. (RoboOlympics `hurdles-freestanding`; the robot as a service: its head cameras, joint sensors and IMUs, and the venue's fixed camera only.)

## Difficulty
A hurdle's top is higher than the robot's hip joints: each of the four has to be jumped with the whole pelvis above 1.067 m and each foot lifted a metre, on a body of 67 kg whose only built-in gait walks at 0.7 m/s and drifts in heading. The hurdles are loose: a clipped bar tips one into the landing, where it lies as an obstacle, and walking up to one shoves it away, which is a failure. In standard mode the agent has no simulator and no state: it sees through the robot's cameras, acts through the client, and has 50 resets (only the last attempt is graded).

## Reference solution
None (as `roboolympics-fun-hurdles-freestanding-i00-privileged`).

## Verification
The sim sidecar's referee judges the live attempt, and that is the verdict (`final.json`: `graded_by` live, `replays` 0;
no grading replay, owner 2026-10-17, board 0151); the frozen start is checked on the live simulator (`instance_mismatch`:
infrastructure), and the video is rendered from the recorded attempt afterwards. `/tests/grade.py` reads
`/data/final.json`: pass iff the live attempt succeeded. The
rules: World Athletics rules for the hurdles, at the indoor distance of 50 m. The lane runs along +x, 1.22 m wide, centred on y = 0; the start line (0.05 m wide) has its far edge at x = 0, the finish line is x = 50.0 m. 4 hurdles stand across the lane, the face of the first one's top bar 13.72 m from the start line and one every 9.14 m after it (the last 8.86 m before the finish). A hurdle is a loose object standing on the track: 1.19 m wide and 1.067 m high, a top bar 0.07 m high, two uprights and a cross bar 0.35 m below its top; its two bases, 0.7 m long, lie on the track on your side of it, with counterweights on them; it weighs 10.9 kg and tips over forwards when a horizontal force of more than 3.8 kg pushes the middle of its top bar (tipped further than about 55 degrees it falls on by itself); it can slide on the track (friction 0.6), fall, bounce and lie where it comes to rest, and you can trip on it, but not climb on it. Each hurdle is judged where it stands at the start (its standing pose on its mark), wherever it is later. The clock starts at t = 0. You go over a hurdle when your pelvis and both your feet (the ankle joints) have crossed the plane of its top bar in its standing pose towards +x, each between the bar's two ends, the pelvis higher than the hurdle's top (1.067 m above the track) and each foot higher than the lower edge of its top bar (1.067 m less 0.07 m), whether the hurdle still stands there or not, and in one flight: from the first of these three crossings to the last, no part of you touches anything but that hurdle's top bar; a pelvis or a foot that comes back over the plane has not crossed it and must cross it again. The hurdles are gone over in order. A hurdle leaves its standing pose when a point of its top bar is more than 0.1 m from where it stood. Knocking a hurdle down with your legs is allowed when a leg strikes its top bar during the clearance: while you touch nothing else but that hurdle's top bar (in the air) or once your pelvis is over the hurdle's plane; a hurdle tipped more than 60.0 degrees is counted as down, without a fault. Where a hurdle comes to rest after that does not matter. You finish at the moment the leading point of your torso (the trunk and head hull; not the arms, legs or feet) reaches the finish line, with all hurdles gone over. You succeed with a finish within 140 s of robot time. A fall does not end the race: you are down while your pelvis is below 0.4 m, you may get up and go on, but 10.0 s down in a row end it. You fail if your pelvis or a foot crosses the next hurdle's plane beside the hurdle, or lower than said above (going under its top); if between the first and the last of these crossings you touch the track or anything else but its top bar (stepping over it); if a part of one of your legs is beside the next hurdle (beyond the bar's ends, within 0.1 m of its plane) lower than the hurdle's top; if a hurdle leaves its standing pose within 0.5 s of being touched by a hand, an arm, your trunk or your pelvis, that touch being your latest on it or within 0.1 s before (knocked by hand or body; also one you have gone over); if you climb on a hurdle you have not yet gone over while it stands (tipped less than 60.0 degrees): it pushes you upwards by more than 100.0 N for longer than 0.15 s, or anything of you but a leg on its top bar touches it at the instant your pelvis or a foot crosses its plane; if a hurdle you have not yet gone over leaves its standing pose in any other way than the legal knock above (pushed, kicked or shoved along the track before you went over it); if your pelvis is more than half the lane's width from y = 0; if your torso reaches the finish line before every hurdle is gone over; when down for 10.0 s; or after 140 s. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §2.7).
