## Task
Throw the discus that your right gripper holds by its rim from inside the circle so that it lands inside the sector at least 15.0 m from the circle, then leave the circle from its rear half and stand. (Embodied Olympics `discus-realgrasp`; the robot as a service: its head cameras, joint sensors and IMUs, and the venue's fixed camera only.)

## Difficulty
15.0 m needs a release at about 12 m/s near 35 to 40 degrees with the discus flat to its flight and spinning, or the air turns it over. The H1-2's arm motors give 40 N m at the shoulder, so the speed has to come from the trunk's turn, inside a circle of 2.5 m that the robot may not leave until the discus is down. At that speed the discus pulls on the hand with some 300 N, and the gripper's friction gives way earlier (measured on the still robot, a steady force at the discus' centre moves it 5 mm in the jaws at about 200 N out of the jaws, 100 N across them and 110 N along the closing axis): the hand's path and the discus' place in the jaws decide whether it stays in until the release. In standard mode the agent has no simulator and no state: it sees through the robot's cameras, acts through the client, and has 50 resets (only the last attempt is graded).

## Verification
The sim sidecar's referee judges the live attempt, and that is the verdict (`final.json`: `graded_by` live, `replays` 0;
no grading replay, owner 2026-10-17); the frozen start is checked on the live simulator (`instance_mismatch`:
infrastructure), and the video is rendered from the recorded attempt afterwards. `/tests/grade.py` reads
`/data/final.json`: pass iff the live attempt succeeded. The
rules: The men's discus (2.0 kg, 0.22 m across, 0.045 m thick at its centre) starts clamped by its rim between the two jaws of your right hand's Dex1-1 gripper (the row's right grip value is the gripper's closure command, 0 open to 1 closed; nothing is welded or grippable: friction alone holds it, and it slips, turns or flies out when the throw pulls harder than the clamp). The circle is 2.5 m across inside its rim; the ground outside is 0.02 m higher than its inside. The sector opens from the circle's centre, 34.92 degrees wide, symmetric about the direction you face at the start. Holding: the discus touches both jaws of the right gripper. Before the release nothing of you but your right hand (the gripper and the wrist) may touch it, and it may not stay on the hand without the gripper's hold (touching the hand but not both jaws) for more than 0.2 s. The release is the gripper's last hold before the discus has touched nothing of you for 0.03 s; there is one release: after it the discus may not be held by the gripper again, nothing of you but the right hand may touch it, and the right hand only during the first 0.2 s (brushing it as it leaves). Its first touch of the ground must be inside the sector lines (on a line is out); the throw is measured from that point to the inside of the rim along the line through the circle's centre. You succeed when a valid throw measures at least 15.0 m and you have then left the circle correctly and stood for 1.0 s. Leaving correctly: after the discus has landed, your first touch of the rim's top or of the ground outside the circle is behind the line through the circle's centre across the throwing direction (the rear half). Standing: only your feet on the ground, the torso within about 20 degrees of upright, the pelvis slower than 0.35 m/s. You fail if any part of you touches the rim's top (its top inside edge included) or the ground outside the circle before the discus has landed, or in the front half before you have left correctly; if the discus is touched by anything but the right hand before the release, stays on the hand without the gripper's hold, is gripped again or touched after the release (but for the right hand's brush); if it lands outside the sector or short of 15.0 m; if you fall (your pelvis lower than 0.5 m above the circle's floor, or any part of you other than your feet on the ground); or after 60 s. Where you touch the ground is judged on the points of contact; a contact within 0.003 m of the rim's top counts as on it.
