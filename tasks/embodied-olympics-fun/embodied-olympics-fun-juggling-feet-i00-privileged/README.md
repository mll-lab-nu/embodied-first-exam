## Task
Juggle the football with your feet: drop it from your hand and keep it in the air for ten foot touches in a row, each one sending the ball up above your knees, without it touching the ground or any other part of you. (Embodied Olympics `juggling-feet`: one Unitree G1 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
Each touch is an upward strike of a foot, timed to a falling 0.43 kg ball and aimed so that the ball comes down on a foot again, ten times in a row, and the robot's thick lower leg leaves only the front few centimetres of the foot where a falling ball meets the foot and not the shin, a spot where a few millimetres decide whether the ball goes up, forward or back onto the leg. To bring a foot under the ball the robot has to stand on the other leg, which neither built-in gait does, and a ball that merely bounces off a still foot loses most of its height and wanders off.

## Reference solution
None. No reference controller exists for this family yet: positive by injection (a real release; the pelvis held where it stands and the ball put back over the toe after each touch; ten real strikes by the right foot's own motors, a lowcmd hip/knee stroke lifting the toe at 0.8 m/s, in/out about 3 m/s, contacts 0.011-0.013 s against a 0.05 s carry limit); negatives: a still raised foot (0.54 of the speed back, not counted), soft strikes, below knee height, the shin above the instep, the thigh, the chest, the head, a hand (also a hand during a foot touch inside the release grace), resting on the foot, trapped between the feet, a re-grip, out of the grid, down. On the G1 a falling ball meets the foot clear of the lower leg's collision hull only on the front 2-3 cm of its top; a foot-following strike controller (pelvis held) lost the ball after two touches; nothing built in stands on one leg.

## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in two fresh processes (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success in both
and both end in the same state. The rules: A size-5 football (0.22 m across, 0.43 kg, in air) starts gripped in your right hand; you stand in the middle of a square grid 5.0 m on each side, marked on the grass. The drop is the moment no hand grips the ball any more; a hand or an arm may still be in contact with the ball for 0.15 s after that (the ball leaving the hand), and the ball cannot be gripped again. From the drop on, only your feet may touch the ball: a foot is the part below the ankle joint (sole, instep, sides, toe and heel); a contact with your lower leg also counts as the foot where it lies over the foot (from 0.08 m behind to 0.15 m ahead of the ankle joint along the foot) and no more than 0.12 m above the ankle joint (along the foot's up direction), because your leg's collision shape covers the instep; the leg above that (shin, knee, thigh), the trunk, the head and the arms are other surfaces. A touch is a contact of the ball with a foot (contacts less than 0.05 s apart are one touch). It counts when, before anything touches the ball again, the ball's centre has risen at least 0.22 m above the height it had when the touch began and the whole ball (its lowest point) has risen above 0.36 m, your knee height; and when the ball left the touch with at least 0.95 of the speed it had when the touch began (both taken at the height where the touch began): a ball that only bounces off a still foot leaves slower, and such a touch is not counted, and is not a fault. You succeed at the moment the tenth touch counts. You fail if the ball touches the ground, if a hand or an arm touches it later than 0.15 s after the drop, or at all while a foot touches it or before a foot's touch has counted or been judged a bounce, if any part of you other than a foot touches it, if both feet touch it at once (the ball trapped), if one touch lasts longer than 0.05 s (the ball resting, rolling or balanced on a foot), if the ball is touched again before a touch has counted or been judged a bounce, if a foot (its ankle) leaves the grid, if you fall (your pelvis lower than 0.35 m above the ground, or any part of you other than the feet on the ground; standing on one foot is not a fall), or after 60 s. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §2.7).

The G1's collision hull of the lower leg is wider and deeper than its shin and covers the instep: a ball lying on the top of the foot touches it. A contact with the lower leg's hull therefore counts as the foot where its point lies over the foot (between 0.08 m behind and 0.15 m ahead of the ankle joint along the foot) and at most 0.12 m above the ankle joint (measured along the foot's up direction: where the ball's equator is when it lies on the instep, plus 1 cm); a contact higher up the leg is the shin, the knee or the thigh: another surface, which the test's count does not allow. · A counter's eye is replaced by numbers: contacts less than 0.05 s apart are one touch, a touch may last at most 0.05 s (a juggle is a strike, the ball may not rest, be cushioned or be balanced on the foot; a real instep kick's contact lasts about 0.01 s, Nunome et al. 2006, J. Sports Sci. 24: 11-22, and the robot's strikes last 0.011-0.013 s), and a touch counts when the ball's centre has then risen at least 0.22 m (one ball diameter) above where it was when the touch began and the whole ball has risen above the robot's knee height (0.36 m above the ground standing), before anything touches it again. · A juggle is a strike: so that a ball that merely bounces off a still foot is not juggling, a touch must send the ball off with at least 0.95 of the speed it arrived with (a still foot returns at most the ball's own restitution, 0.85, and a raised leg held by its motors about 0.55); a touch that does not is not counted, and is not a fault. · The ball's contact with the foot keeps the ball's own restitution (0.85, from the rule book's drop test): the robot's foot is rigid, like the metal pendulum with which Andersen, Kristensen and Sorensen (Football Science 5: 45-50, 2008) measured 0.82, and the compliance of a human foot and ankle (whose kicks they and earlier studies measured at 0.45-0.65) is here the compliance of the robot's own joints: a raised leg held by its motors returns about 0.55 of the ball's speed. · The ball starts in the robot's right hand, held by the simulator's grip (the robot has fists, no fingers); the drop is the opening of the grip.
