# Robot badminton: program play (match-wallclock)

You play robot badminton against a scripted opponent (you are seat `A`). Robot singles badminton on a regulation court in real time (slowed down): a Franka Panda arm with a racket on an x-y sliding base, a point-mass shuttle with high quadratic drag (terminal speed 6.8 m/s) and no bounce, a racket contact law; first to 5 points, rally scoring; the players' robots serve (the rally winner serves next): the server tosses the shuttle (from rest or rising up to 0.5 m) at a point it chooses and hits it underhand, below 1.15 m, into the diagonal service court; format match-wallclock: you against a scripted computer opponent (levels 1-2, `game spec` match.opponent_level), format duel-wallclock: two players.
This task is generated from `sports/badminton/sport.toml` (task `badminton-match-wallclock-program-standard`, RoboOlympics).

## Time
The world runs in real time at 1/S of real speed (S = `timing.slowdown` in `game spec`); your thinking costs game time. At the end of every unit the world freezes for the reviews.

Whenever the world freezes for a review (after every unit: a point, a shot, a heat; see `game spec`), send `game ready "WHY" "PLAN"` (a short review) before the next unit starts.
The match is graded when your session ends: stay until it is over. **If your session ends before the match is over, you lose the match** (a valid result, success 0), also if you never sent `game start`.

## Write a program
You may write a program that observes, measures in the images, computes your next action and sends it, without a
new model response for each action. You stay in the loop: watch its log, and stop, fix and restart it whenever
needed. Only you send `game start` and `game ready`.

- Use only the camera images, the service's replies and this document; no simulator internals or hidden state.
- One action at a time: an action sent before the previous one has returned is refused (HTTP 429).
- Your container: Python 3.12 with numpy, SciPy and Pillow; no internet except your model's API. Keep your scripts, logs
  and notes in `/app/output/`.

## Interface
Use the `game` command, or the same calls as HTTP with JSON at `$SIM_URL/<SEAT>/` (SIM_URL = http://localhost:8000, your seat is `$SEAT`):

| `game` command | HTTP | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `game spec` | `GET /spec` | the actions (types, fields, ranges), the cameras with their intrinsics, the rule constants |
| `game status` | `GET /status` | the current state, no image |
| `game start` | `POST /start {}` | you are ready; the match begins when every seat has started |
| `game wait` | `POST /wait {"timeout_s": 60}` | block until it is your turn (or the match is over) |
| `game observe` | `POST /observe {}` | the state and the camera images (one PNG of tiles: `observation.tiles`) |
| `game act JSON` | `POST /act JSON` | one action |
| `game none ` | `POST /act {"type":"none", ...}` | one action (`game spec` lists the fields and ranges) |
| `game ready "WHY" "PLAN"` | `POST /ready {"why":"...","plan":"..."}` | your review after each of your actions |

Errors: 400 = an invalid action (nothing happens), 409 = not now (not your turn, or a review is due), 429 = one
action in flight.

## Rules
- robot singles badminton on a regulation court in real time (slowed down): a Franka Panda arm with a racket on an x-y sliding base, a point-mass shuttle with high quadratic drag (terminal speed 6.8 m/s) and no bounce, a racket contact law; first to 5 points, rally scoring; the players' robots serve (the rally winner serves next): the server tosses the shuttle (from rest or rising up to 0.5 m) at a point it chooses and hits it underhand, below 1.15 m, into the diagonal service court; format match-wallclock: you against a scripted computer opponent (levels 1-2, `game spec` match.opponent_level), format duel-wallclock: two players.
- `match`: first to 5 points (rally scoring); from 4-4 a player must lead by 2, and at 6-6 the next point wins (at most 7); the match also ends after 300 hits (both sides) or 40 points played (replayed lets included): then the player with more points wins, at equal points the player who won the last point
- `play`: there is no bounce: hit the shuttle before it reaches the floor. Your shot is good if your racket meets the shuttle once, on your half, and the shuttle passes over the net and lands in your opponent's singles court (lines are in); after your hit your racket no longer touches it. You lose the point if your shot goes into the net (crosses the net plane below the top), comes down on your own side or lands out, or if a shuttle that is yours to hit lands in your court; an opponent's shot that would land out of your court wins you the point, so you may let it go. Serves (BWF Law 9): the winner of a rally serves the next point (after a let the same server; state.you_serve, state.serve). Before every serve both robots are put at rest in the ready pose at their spots: x = +0.3 in the right service court or -0.3 in the left one, y = -4.0 (your frame); both stand in their right courts when the server's score is even, else in their left ones (diagonally opposite); the spot is your base's home for the point. From 1 s after play resumes (the receiver is ready; state.serve.phase 'open') the server has 3 s to toss the shuttle with {'type':'toss','pos':[X,Y,Z],'height':H}: when the command takes effect (the same lead as an act) the shuttle appears at that point (0.5 <= Z <= 1.8, within 1.0 m horizontally of your base, Y <= -0.5) and is released from rest (H = 0 or omitted) or rises H straight up (0 <= H <= 0.5) before it falls (until then it is held beside the server). Then hit it with your racket (an act, which you may send before the toss): the serve is good if your racket meets the shuttle below 1.15 m, the shuttle leaves the racket upwards, passes over the net and lands in the receiver's diagonally opposite service court (between its short service line and its back line, lines in) or is returned. It is the server's fault (the receiver's point) if it does not toss the shuttle in time (undue delay; the third time disqualifies the player: it loses the match), misses the shuttle, hits it above 1.15 m or not upwards, or the serve hits the net or lands outside that court. Both bases must stay within 5 cm of their spots until the serve is hit (or faulted): a base moving earlier is that player's fault (both at once: a let, replayed)
- `opponent`: format match-wallclock: a scripted computer opponent. You serve the first point; the opponent serves when it won the last rally: from its service court at 1.05 m, upwards, high and deep or short into your diagonal service court, always within your reach (level 1: 70 % high serves, towards the middle of the court; level 2: 50 % high, wider and closer to the short service line); it stands still in its service court while you serve. It never makes an unforced error: it returns every shuttle it can reach, into your court and within your reach; shuttles far from it or fast beat it; level 2 reacts and moves faster and reaches further than level 1
- `world`: metres; your own frame: x to your right, y towards the opponent, z up. Floor z = 0; singles court |x| <= 2.59, |y| <= 6.70 (lines in); the net is the plane y = 0 with its top at z = 1.524 (posts at x = +-3.05); short service lines at |y| = 1.98; the centre line x = 0 divides each half into a right and a left service court. Your half is y < 0
- `robot`: a Franka Panda arm on a 1.0 m pedestal whose base slides on rails (|bx| <= 3.25, -7.5 <= by <= -0.5, up to 6 m/s and 20 m/s^2; home: the point's spot (+-0.3, -4.0), see play); the racket head centre is 0.56 m beyond the flange; the string face is a disc of radius 0.14 m. The racket meets shuttles 0.4-2.5 m above the floor; overhead contacts (1.8-2.5 m) allow the fastest, steepest shots
- `shuttle`: a point mass under gravity (9.81 m/s^2) and quadratic air drag, dv/dt = g - K |v| v with K = 0.212 per metre (terminal speed 6.8 m/s): hit at 40 m/s it slows to about 10 m/s within 0.3 s, and a high shuttle falls almost vertically at close to 6.8 m/s. No spin, no wind, no bounce
- `racket_contact`: the racket meets the shuttle when the shuttle's centre crosses the string plane within 0.14 m of the head centre; the shuttle leaves exactly along the face normal n (the side the head moves towards, relative to the shuttle) at the speed 20 max(0, v_head.n) (1 - 0.3 (r/0.14)^2) + 0.15 max(0, -v_in.n), where v_head is the head velocity, r the contact's distance from the head centre and v_in the shuttle's incoming velocity; the rest of the head and incoming velocity is lost. A head speed along the normal of 0.5 m/s gives about 10 m/s, 2 m/s about 40 m/s; the arm manages up to about 2.4 m/s
- `act`: high level: {'type':'act','t':T,'pos':[PX,PY,PZ],'normal':[NX,NY,NZ],'vel':[VX,VY,VZ]}: the racket's state at contact, T s after the act takes effect: the head centre, the string-face normal and the head velocity (m/s). Limits: 0 < T <= 5, |x| <= 3.4, -7.5 <= y <= -0.3, 0.2 <= z <= 3.0, speed <= 8 m/s. The act takes effect `act.lead_steps` control steps after the step at which it arrives (`game spec`; the stroke is planned meanwhile) and replaces the stroke under way; until then the robot keeps its current command. The controller drives the base to stand for a forehand at the contact point (or as far as it gets in time) and swings the arm under the joint speed limits; it knows nothing about the shuttle. The delivered state scatters by about 6 mm, 0.8 degrees and 3 % (seeded; format duel-wallclock: both robots draw from one sequence, the n-th act of each robot gets the same scatter). {'type':'none'} cancels the stroke (the robot returns to its ready pose, the base to its spot), with the same lead; {'type':'toss','pos':[X,Y,Z],'height':H} tosses the shuttle for your serve (see play), with the same lead
- `lowlevel`: low level: {'type':'traj','q':[[base_x, base_y, q1..q7], ...]}: joint targets, one per 10 ms control step from the next step on (then the robot returns to its ready pose and the base to its spot), each within the joint ranges and speed limits of `game spec` joints; none and serve as above, at the next step
- `observation`: a PNG of 2 rows x 4 columns of 480 x 360 tiles: row 1 a camera behind and above your base line looking down the court, row 2 a camera at the side of the court (x < 0) looking along +x (your half on the right); the columns are frames 60 ms apart, newest on the right, with each frame's step and game time (view_steps, view_sim_times). No shuttle coordinates: the shuttle is drawn enlarged, an orange cork (12 cm ball centred on its position) with a white skirt behind it. `game spec` gives both cameras' poses and intrinsics (pinhole, 45 degree vertical field of view, focal length 434.6 px, principal point at the tile centre). The state gives your robot (robot_base_xy, q_arm, racket_pos, racket_normal), what you heard (tosses, serves and hits, with their times), whether the shuttle is coming to you, and the serve: you_serve, serve.court (right or left), serve.phase (waiting: the receiver is getting ready; open: the server may toss; tossed; done: the serve was delivered), serve.opens_in_s and serve.deadline_in_s for the server, serve.tossed_at_step; a point lost by a service fault says 'serve_fault:<reason>: ...' (undue_delay, missed, too_high, not_upwards, net, out, moved), the receiver's early move 'receive_fault:moved: ...'
- `duel`: format duel-wallclock: another agent controls an identical robot across the net; in each player's frame its own half is y < 0. match.first_server serves the first point; then the winner of each rally serves (both players serve by the same rules, see play)
- Winner: a player disqualified for repeated undue delay (its third serve not tossed in time) loses; else first to 5 points (from 4-4 by 2, at most 7); at the match limit (300 hits or 40 points played) the player with more points; at equal points the player who won the last point that was not a let; then (last resort, not from the rulebook) the player who received first (format match-wallclock: the opponent). There are no draws.
- Deviation D1 (rulebook 7): one short game: first to 5 points, from 4-4 a two-point lead is needed, at most 7 (6-6: the next point wins) instead of best of three games to 21 (cap 30); the match also ends after 300 hits (both sides) or 40 points played (lets included): the player ahead wins, at equal points the winner of the last point
- Deviation D2 (rulebook 9.1.5-9.1.8): the robot has no free hand to hold and drop the shuttle: the server tosses it with the command toss {pos: [x, y, z], height: H} (the common racket-sport toss, docs/conventions/racket_serve_zh.md): when the command takes effect the shuttle appears at that point (0.5-1.8 m high, within 1.0 m horizontally of the server's base, on its half) and is released from rest (H = 0) or projected straight up to rise H (at most 0.5 m; the Laws do not forbid a small toss); until then it is held beside the server. The toss has no spin (a point mass has none, Law 9.1.5). Judged: the server's racket must hit it (a miss is a fault, Law 9.1.8), with the shuttle below 1.15 m at the hit (Law 9.1.6, judged at the shuttle's point: it has no size) and leaving the racket upwards (Law 9.1). Not judged: the racket hitting the base of the shuttle first and moving forwards throughout the service (Laws 9.1.5, 9.1.7)
- Deviation D3 (rulebook 13.3-13.4): the shuttle is a point mass: a crossing of the net plane below the net top (between the posts) is a fault of the hitter; touching the net or reaching over it is not judged; a racket meets the shuttle only on its own half and only when it is that player's turn (no double hits)
- Deviation D4 (rulebook 9.1.3-9.1.4): before every serve the referee puts both robots at rest in the ready pose at a fixed spot in their service courts (diagonally opposite: the right courts when the server's score is even, Law 10.1): x = +0.3 (right court) or -0.3 (left court), y = -4.0 in each robot's frame; the spot is the robot's home for the point (where its base returns between strokes). Standing still (Law 9.1.4) is judged on the base: a base more than 5 cm from its spot before the serve is delivered (hit, or faulted) is that player's fault; both at once: a let (Law 14.2.2). The base moves within |x| <= 3.25, -7.5 <= y <= -0.5 at up to 6 m/s and 20 m/s^2
- Deviation D5 (rulebook -): the last resort of the no-draw rule (equal points at the match limit and no point won) is not from the rulebook: the player who received first wins (format match-wallclock: the opponent)
- Deviation D6 (rulebook 9.1.1, 16.6-16.7): undue delay has a fixed limit: the server must toss the shuttle within 3 s after the receiver is ready (1 s after play resumes, Law 9.4), else it is a service fault (the receiver's point); a player's third such fault disqualifies it (it loses the match) instead of the umpire's warning and the referee's discretion (Laws 16.7.1-16.7.2)
- Deviation D7 (rulebook 6): no toss: format duel-wallclock: the runner plays both orders of the first server (match.first_server); format match-wallclock: you serve the first point

## Geometry and physics
Everything you need is in `game spec`: the world frame, the release point, the cameras (pose, field of view, size,
`focal_px`, `principal_point_px`; pinhole: u = cx + f * x_right / depth, v = cy - f * y_up / depth) and the
execution scatter. The physics engine is mujoco (robots, PD-controlled joints at 1 ms) + a point-mass shuttle with quadratic drag integrated at 1 ms (sim/bm.py).
