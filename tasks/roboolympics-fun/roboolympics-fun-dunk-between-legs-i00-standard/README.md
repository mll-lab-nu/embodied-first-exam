## Task
From the top of the key, with the basketball pressed between your two palms, dribble it, make one of its bounces pass between your legs, gather it in both hands, and with at most two steps dunk it: force it down through the ring from above with your hands on it, then land on your feet. (RoboOlympics `dunk-between-legs`; the robot as a service: its head cameras, joint sensors and IMUs, and the venue's fixed camera only.)

## Difficulty
Every touch of the dribble has to push the ball down with a flat palm, and one bounce has to go through the narrow gap between the robot's own feet and come out where a hand can meet it, without touching a leg; then both palms have to catch the moving ball and squeeze it, with only the arms' force holding it. After at most two steps the 67 kg body has to rise about a metre with the ball pressed overhead and bring it down into a ring only 0.21 m wider than the ball, and land; the robot has a walking gait (which steps on the spot even at a zero command) and nothing that jumps or lands. In standard mode the agent has no simulator and no state: it sees through the robot's cameras, acts through the client, and has 50 resets (only the last attempt is graded).

## Reference solution
None (as `roboolympics-fun-dunk-between-legs-i00-privileged`).

## Verification
The sim sidecar's referee judges the live attempt, and that is the verdict (`final.json`: `graded_by` live, `replays` 0;
no grading replay, owner 2026-10-17, board 0151); the frozen start is checked on the live simulator (`instance_mismatch`:
infrastructure), and the video is rendered from the recorded attempt afterwards. `/tests/grade.py` reads
`/data/final.json`: pass iff the live attempt succeeded. The
rules: A FIBA court (15.0 m wide inside its side lines, lines 0.05 m wide); the official basket: a rigid ring of 0.45 m inner diameter (0.018 m metal), its top edge 3.05 m above the floor, its centre 0.375 m in front of a backboard 1.8 m wide and 1.05 m high whose lower edge is 2.9 m above the floor. The ball is a size-7 basketball (0.239 m across, 0.62 kg, it keeps 0.85 of its speed in a bounce on the floor). Your hands have no fingers and no grip: the ball starts pressed between your two flat palms, and only the force of your arms and friction hold it; it can slip out. A hand is the palm pad and the wrist; a hand is on the ball while it touches the ball, gaps shorter than 0.03 s included; the ball is in your hands while at least one hand is on it. Starting the dribble: you stand still with the ball in your hands; the first foot you lift makes the other your pivot foot; the pivot foot, and before a foot is lifted either foot, may not slide more than 0.2 m along the floor, and the pivot foot may not leave the floor before the ball has left your hands (a foot has left the floor when it has not touched it for 0.03 s). The ball may not touch the floor while it is in your hands. The dribble starts when the ball has left your hands and touched the floor. The dribble: you play the ball by touches of one hand at a time that push it to the floor: between two touches by your hands the ball must have touched the floor (the ball leaving your hands at the start counts as a touch); contacts of your hands less than 0.03 s apart are one touch; a touch of one hand may last at most 0.3 s (longer: the ball has come to rest in the hand); the ball may touch no part of you but your hands; its centre must stay within 2.0 m (horizontally) of your pelvis; there is no limit to your steps. Between the legs: the leg gate is the triangle whose corners are the soles of your two feet (each foot's sole point) and your crotch (the point midway between your two hip joints), wherever your legs are, up to the height of your lower knee joint, while the triangle's area is at least 0.02 m2 (a thinner one, with a sole on the line from the other sole to the crotch, is no gate). A bounce goes between your legs when, between two touches of your hands, the ball touches the floor, its centre passes through the leg gate from one side of the gate's plane to the other while no hand touches it, the ball itself (not you) moving through that plane in the direction of the pass at 1.0 m/s or more, this is the last time its centre passes the gate's plane before the next touch, and at that next touch the ball is on the other side of the plane from where it was at the touch before. At least one bounce of your dribble must go between your legs. The gather: the dribble ends when both your hands are on the ball at the same moment; it must end so, and only after a bounce between your legs. From then on the ball must stay in your hands until its centre has gone down into the ring, and it may not touch the floor (a second dribble). Travelling: after the gather you may take at most 2 steps with the ball in your hands. A step is a foot touching the floor after it has been off it for at least 0.03 s; both feet landing within 0.03 s of each other are one step; the same foot may not touch down twice in a row; a foot on the floor may not slide more than 0.2 m from where it stood or was put down; after that no foot may touch the floor again until the ball is through the ring. The dunk: the ball's centre goes down through the ring's plane inside the ring while both your hands are on the ball, or while a hand is on it whose latest contact with it is at least 0.06 m above the ball's centre (pressing it down from above), and on down to one ball diameter below the ring (in the hands, or let go once its centre is inside the ring). A ball whose centre comes up through the ring from below is a violation. Hanging on the ring: you may touch the basket (the ring, its plate, the backboard) with any part of you for at most 0.3 s in all. After the dunk let go of the ball (no hand on it) and stand: for 1.0 s without a break only your feet touch the ground, your torso is upright (the cosine of its tilt at least 0.94) and your pelvis moves slower than 0.35 m/s. Then you have succeeded. You fail if you travel; if the ball touches the floor while in your hands before the dribble, or goes through the ring before it; if during the dribble a hand touches the ball twice without the ball touching the floor in between, a touch of one hand lasts too long, the ball touches a part of you other than a hand, or the ball gets further from you than the control distance; if you gather the ball before a bounce between your legs; if after the gather the ball leaves your hands before its centre has entered the ring or touches the floor; if the ball goes through the ring after an entry at which neither both hands nor a hand from above were on it (a shot, a tip from below or the side, a drop); if, let go inside the ring, it touches the floor without having gone through; if the ball comes up through the ring from below; if the ball or any part of you touches the floor on or outside a boundary line (the end line or a side line); if you hang on the ring; if you fall at any time (a fall: your pelvis lower than 0.35 m above the floor, or any part of you other than your feet on the floor); or after 40 s. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §2.7).

| Rule book | FIBA Official Basketball Rules 2024: Art. 2 (court), Art. 3 and the Basketball Equipment appendix (backboard, ring, size-7 ball), Art. 13 (how the ball is played), Art. 14 (control: a player holds a live ball), Art. 15 (a dunk: the ball forced downwards into the basket with one or both hands), Art. 16 (goal; a ball that passes through the basket from below is a violation), Art. 23 (out of bounds), Art. 24 (dribbling: its start, its end, no second dribble), Art. 25 (travelling, 25.2.1.2: two steps upon completion of a dribble), Art. 26 (three seconds), Art. 36 (technical foul: hanging on the ring); deviations: Art. 3 (equipment): the hands are flat rubber palm pads without fingers; the ball is held only by pressing it between the two palms (friction), and it slips or drops when the arms do not press hard enough. It is dribbled by pushing it with a palm. · Art. 3 (equipment, the hand): the palm is modelled as the soft part of a hand: it absorbs the ball (a ball thrown at a still palm keeps 0.12 of its speed) instead of bouncing it like the floor (0.85); with the ball's own floor rebound on the palms a squeezed ball rattles out of them within about a second. A dribbling hand therefore gives the ball its speed by its own motion. The floor, the ring and the backboard keep the ball's rule-book rebound. · Art. 24.1.2 (the ball 'comes to rest' in a hand) is read as: a touch of one hand that lasts longer than 0.3 s. The dribble ends when both hands touch the ball at the same time (the gather); a dribble ended by a ball at rest in one hand ends the attempt (no dribble can be started again, Art. 24.2). · Art. 13: during the dribble every touch of the ball by a part of you other than a hand is a violation; intent (an accidental touch of a leg is legal in the rule book) cannot be judged. · The rule book has no 'between the legs': this task's definition (the leg gate: the triangle of your two feet and your crotch below your knees, crossed by the ball's centre during a bounce, no hand on it, the ball moving through it at 1.0 m/s or more, and the ball on the other side at the next touch) is this task's own requirement. · Art. 15 defines a dunk only as forcing the ball downwards into the basket with one or both hands: here, at the moment its centre enters the ring, both hands are on the ball, or a hand is on it whose latest contact is at least 0.06 m above its centre (a flat palm pressing it from above). · Art. 14 / 24 give no distance for 'control' of a dribbled ball: here the ball's centre must stay within 2.0 m (horizontally) of your pelvis while it is dribbled. · Basketball Equipment (basket): the ring is rigid (no pressure release) and the net is only drawn: it does not slow the ball. The ball is through the basket when its centre has gone down through the ring's plane inside the ring and on to one ball diameter below it. · Art. 26 (three seconds) is not applied: the attempt is a single action without opponents (as in the other basketball families, which start in or next to the restricted area). · Art. 36: hanging on the ring is judged by time: the robot may touch the basket (ring, its plate, the backboard) for at most 0.3 s in all. · The landing (on the feet, standing 1.0 s) is this task's own requirement; the rule book asks nothing of a player after a goal. |
