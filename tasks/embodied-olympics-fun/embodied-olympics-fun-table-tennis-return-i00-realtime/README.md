## Task
Return the service: after the table tennis ball has bounced once on your court, strike it with the paddle held in your right hand so that it touches the opponent's court, without letting go of the paddle. (Embodied Olympics `table-tennis-return`; the robot as a service on a clock that does not stop, slowdown S = 1: its head cameras, joint sensors and IMUs, and the venue's fixed camera only.)

## Difficulty
The served ball is over your court for a few tenths of a second, low above a table that is as high as your hips, and may have to be met far inside the end line: the blade, a rigid extension of a 0.5 m arm, has to be there at the right instant and angle while the body leans over the table without touching the net. The return must clear a 15 cm net and still come down on a court 1.37 m long, off a ball that carries spin. The paddle sits in a three-finger hand with small finger motors: the blade is where the fingers hold it, and a fast stroke must not fling it out of the hand. The scene is dynamic (A ball machine serves the ball toward the robot at serve_time_s.), and in realtime mode time does not stop: once an attempt's clock starts the venue's timed events happen whatever the agent does, so it has to plan or stream the robot's rows from a program of its own, with no simulator and no state, and 5 resets (only the last attempt is graded).

## Reference solution
None (as `embodied-olympics-fun-table-tennis-return-i00-privileged`).

## Verification
The sim sidecar (`rcb_realtime.py` + `rcb_service.py`, the shared eai-standard/2.3 layer, with the standard twin's
`server.py`) runs the attempt on its clock and its referee judges it live; every control step the simulator applied is
recorded, and the live referee's call on the last attempt is the verdict (`final.json`: `graded_by` live, `replays` 0; no
grading replay, owner 2026-10-17); the video is rendered from the recorded attempt afterwards.
`/tests/grade.py` reads `/data/final.json`: pass iff the live attempt succeeded. The clock's record
(`stalls`, `max_lag_s`, ...) is copied into `reward.json` and decides nothing. `nop`: the attempt never starts (its
clock starts at the first submit), reward 0. The
rules: The table is the official one: 2.74 m long, 1.525 m wide, its playing surface 0.76 m above the floor, with a net 0.1525 m high across its middle that reaches 0.1525 m beyond each side line. Your court is the half of the playing surface on your side of the net, the opponent's court the other half; the top edges belong to the playing surface, the sides of the table top do not (a ball that touches the table with its centre below the level of the playing surface has hit the side). The ball is the official one (40 mm, 2.7 g) and flies in air: drag and the lift of its spin act on it. The racket is a separate body: a rigid square blade 0.15 m wide and 0.015 m thick on a straight handle 0.1 m long, 0.032 m wide and 0.025 m thick, 0.14 kg in all, the blade's centre 0.175 m from the butt. At the start its handle is closed in your right hand (three fingers: a thumb, an index and a middle finger), which holds it by contact and friction only: it can slip, turn in the hand and fall. Your right hand is the racket hand, your left hand the free hand. The racket is in your hand while its handle touches the hand's fingers and its thumb or palm; it has left your hand when for more than 0.05 s it has not. The service is thrown by a ball machine about 3.0 s after the start (it is served when it has left the machine's wheels): a legal service, so the ball bounces first on the server's court, passes the net and bounces on your court. Then you must make a good return (Law 2.7): strike the ball, after that one bounce on your court, with the blade of the racket in your hand or with the racket hand below the wrist, so that it touches the opponent's court, directly or after touching the net; it may pass over or around the net. You succeed at the moment the ball touches the opponent's court after your strike. All contacts of the blade or the racket hand with the ball within 0.05 s of the first are one strike. You fail if the racket leaves your hand at any time (dropped, thrown or slipped out of the hand); if the racket touches any part of you other than the racket hand; if you or the racket touch the ball before the service, or before it has bounced on your court (obstruction); if the ball bounces a second time on your court before you strike it (ball contacts with the table less than 0.02 s apart are one bounce); if anything other than the blade or the racket hand touches the ball (the racket's handle, an arm, the body); if the blade or the racket hand touches the ball again later than 0.05 s after the first contact (struck twice, or carried); if after your strike the ball touches your own court, the side of the table or the floor before the opponent's court; if the ball touches the floor at any time; if any part of you or the racket touches the net (it gives a little when touched); if the racket touches the floor; if your free hand touches the playing surface while the ball is in play (from the service on); if you fall; or after 8 s. Anything else of you, the racket included, may touch the table. A fall is your pelvis lower than 0.35 m above the floor, or any part of you other than your feet touching the floor. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §4).
