## Task
Drop in from the start platform, ride the in-run, take off from the kicker, do a straight air in the air, land on your skis and ride away past the finish line. (Embodied Olympics `freeski-straight-air`: one Unitree G1 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
The robot must start itself from a standstill on level snow, ride a 35 degree in-run to about 16 m/s and leave a 25 degree kicker with the right rotation for its trick, finish the rotation in about 1.5 s of air and land on a 36 degree slope with no balance controller of its own.

## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in one fresh process (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success. The rules: FIS big air, one jump, judged on one trick: the straight air: no spin (your skis turn less than 90 degrees about the vertical in the air) and no flip. Your feet are bound to freestyle skis (1.35 m twin tips). You stand on the level start platform with your boots 0.3 m behind its edge (the start line); the start signal is given at t = 0 and you must leave the platform by your own action (nothing holds or pushes you) within 20.0 s. The course: the platform's edge rounds onto the in-run (38.0 m at 35.0 degrees), a transition and a 5.0 m flat, then the kicker (it rises to its lip at 25.0 degrees, 2.1 m above the flat), a table 15.0 m long at the flat's level, the knoll, the landing (36.0 degrees, 30.0 m long), a transition and the flat outrun. The jump is your flight off the kicker: it starts within 4.0 m of the lip, rising at 10.0 degrees or more. The trick is measured in the air only (from the moment your last contact with the snow ends until the next contact). The spin is your skis' heading change: each ski's long axis (tail to tip) is followed in the horizontal plane (unwrapped, so whole turns add up; held while the ski points within 10 degrees of the vertical) and the spin is the mean of the two skis' changes, counted in half turns of 180 degrees (your pelvis's or trunk's own yaw does not count). The flip is your pelvis's rotation about the horizontal axis across your take-off direction, after removing the 61.0 degrees a straight air turns forward anyway, counted in full turns of 360 degrees and only if your body went upside down; each to the nearest unit (up to half a unit short or over still counts as that unit). A flip with half a turn of spin is still that flip. You succeed when the jump's rotation is the straight air and the landing is clean: the first contact after the flight is your skis', on the knoll or the landing hill (past the table), with both skis pointing forwards (down the course, +x) within 45.0 degrees at that first contact; from 0.5 s after it until both boots cross the finish line each ski's heading stays within 90.0 degrees of its heading at that moment (no spinning on the snow); from the first contact until both boots cross the finish line (15.0 m past the start of the outrun) no part of you but the boots touches the snow and you are not down for 1.0 s in all (down: on the snow with your pelvis less than 0.4 m above it, measured along its normal, or your trunk more than 70.0 degrees from its normal; the time down shrinks again at a quarter of the rate while you are up); and at the finish line your pelvis is at least 0.4 m above the snow and both skis point forwards (within 30.0 degrees). You fail: on any other trick (more or less rotation, a flip where none is asked, a different direction of flip, a take-off riding backwards), a landing with a ski pointing more than 45.0 degrees from forwards, a spin on the snow (a ski's heading more than 90.0 degrees from where it was 0.5 s after the landing), on a fall at any time (a part of you other than your boots on the snow, or down for 1.0 s in all), a landing on the table, the body first, a run with no jump, a stop (slower than 0.3 m/s for 3.0 s after the start), no start within 20.0 s, or at 40 s. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §4).
