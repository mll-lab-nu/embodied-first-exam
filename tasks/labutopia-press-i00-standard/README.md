## Task
LabUtopia's `press` (Press) on the same frozen instance as `labutopia-press-i00-privileged`: Press the red button on the instrument's front panel (the two grey buttons next to it do not count) and keep it pressed. Success the moment its cap has been pushed in more than 5 mm for one second (60 control steps in a row). The panel is vertical and faces the arm: the button is pushed in along the forward (+x) axis of the arm's base.
In front of the arm stands an instrument with a vertical front panel that carries three square buttons in a row, each 8 cm across: one red, two dark grey. Standard mode (eai-standard/2.2): the agent drives the robot through a client in one episode, with only its proprioception and the scene's cameras.

## Difficulty
Same instance and success rule as `labutopia-press-i00-privileged` (LabUtopia's level 1). The target is one of three identical buttons told apart by colour only, on a vertical panel at arm's length; it has to be held in for a full second. In standard mode the agent loses resets, the simulator and the state of the objects: it sees camera images (glassware is nearly transparent in them and absent from the depth maps) and the robot's proprioception, acts in joint target chunks, and gets one episode, so it has to find the things it handles in the images and recover from its own mistakes without starting over.

## Reference solution
`solution/oracle_stream.py` streams the privileged reference's rows (`solution/oracle.npz`, 328 rows, float32) through the client as `joint_position_velocity` chunks; it uses no privileged information at run time. The service's recorded trajectory is then exactly the privileged reference: success after 328 control steps.

## Verification
The sim service judges the episode live and records every executed control step; the collect hook (`environment/sim/finalize.sh`) ends the service, replays the recorded trajectory in two fresh processes and writes `final.json`; `tests/grade.py` (separate sandbox, no network) passes iff the live episode succeeded, both replays succeed, and they end in the same state. A missing or unfinished `final.json` (the hook never wrote its verdict) and a replay that gave no result are `infra_error = 1`, never the agent's 0. `nop`: the episode times out, reward 0.

## Compute resources
Minimum allocation for one trial (docs/PROTOCOL.md §2.9; `[metadata.resources]` in `task.toml`). The agent phase counts every container of the trial at once (the agent's, and in standard modes the simulator sidecar); the verifier phase runs after it.

| Memory | 28 GB | 27 GB |

- GPU: required, an RTX-class NVIDIA GPU (ray-tracing cores, for Isaac Sim / Omniverse RTX rendering); recommended: RTX 4090 24GB / L40S 48GB.
- Measured: oracle checks on RTX 4090 (driver 580), lab_001 pick: rendering container peak 17 GB RAM, about 7 GB GPU, 8 CPUs (start-up bursts to 16 cores for about 70 s); author: lab_003 families 21 GB at the peak (8 vCPU host)
- Limits in the agent phase (docs/PROTOCOL.md §2.10; `[metadata.resources.limits]`, shown to the agent in `instruction.md`; always measured and reported, and exceeding one fails the trial only when the run sets `RCB_LIMITS_ENFORCE=1`), the agent's container: no GPU, 8 GiB memory, 512 processes, 20 GiB of new files under `/app`, `/tmp`, `/root`; 60 s within 120 s. Expected peak of the whole trial: 16 GiB GPU memory, 28 GiB memory. Limits measured: GPU: about 7 GiB, oracle on an RTX 4090 (author); no trial data -> max(1.5 x peak, peak + 4), at least 16 for Isaac Sim; memory: no samples of the agent's container; floor 8 GiB (at most the 32 GiB cap); whole trial: + the sim service's 20 GiB (docker peak 12.7 GiB, 1 samples); processes 512 and disk 20 GiB: design defaults, not yet measured.
